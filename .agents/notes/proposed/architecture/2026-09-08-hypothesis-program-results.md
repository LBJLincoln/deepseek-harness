# Agent Note: What the hypothesis program measured on the operator's subscription

Status: proposed

English | [中文](2026-09-08-hypothesis-program-results.zh.md)

## Problem

The hypothesis program ([slate](2026-09-07-hypothesis-program.md)) promised that every experiment would end with a verdict and an interval recorded under `data/proving-ground/`, and that a refutation would be recorded with the same care as a confirmation. Two days of runs on the operator's Claude Code subscription produced thirty records and eleven folds; nothing summarizes them, so a reader who wants to know whether the harness loop beats the product's own loop, whether a smaller model is worse, whether the directive loop earns its attempts, whether a knowledge pack changes anything, and what a router could gain has to read the proving-ground README end to end. This note is that summary, with the refutations first and the noise floor stated before any delta.

## Proposal

Read the records as follows, adopt the readings as the current state of knowledge about this harness on these tasks, and queue the slices the results demand rather than any change the results do not support.

### The instrument, and what it can tell apart

Every number below comes from a frozen paired experiment or a fleet run on `examples/headless-agent/tests/fixtures/proving-ground-bench/`: thirty zero-dependency program tasks in tiers 2 to 4 plus ten tier-5 tasks whose hidden cases the validator holds, eight of them audited so that every hidden case is derivable from the prompt. A cell is one task, one repetition, one arm; the runner validates the workspace against the task's checks after each attempt, issues a directive clustered by failing channel, and certifies when every case passes. Since 09:25 UTC on 2026-09-08 every cell is sealed: bubblewrap mounts an empty parent over the run directory with only the cell's workspace bound back in, the read barrier denies the parent and grants the workspace, and reads the barrier refuses are counted on the run. Two facts about the instrument bound every reading:

- **The noise floor is one flip in sixteen.** The middle product model ran the same sixteen sealed tier-5 cells twice at seed 2 (the twentieth and twenty-first records); the runs agree on fifteen cells ([fold](../../../../data/proving-ground/folds/2026-09-08-sonnet-repeatability-t5-seed2.json)). Against an unsealed run of the same arm the agreement is fourteen and thirteen of sixteen, with one task (conf-canon) certified twice in both sealed runs and never in the unsealed one. A paired delta of 0.0625 on sixteen cells is inside one arm's own run-to-run variation; a delta has to be three flips wide before it says anything.
- **The arms of an experiment run one after the other, not interleaved.** The experiments service runs the whole baseline arm and then the whole candidate arm, so an arm is confounded with its hour on the shared subscription. Seed pairing does not remove this. A cell-interleaved schedule is queued below.

### What was refuted

- **The harness loop loses to the product loop on the same model.** Refuted on tiers 2 to 4: 48 of 48 against 48 of 48, level wall time. On tier 5 the two loops are inside the noise floor of each other: 12 against 11 unsealed and uncapped, 15 against 14 sealed under the same caps, where the product loop's two misses each breached a cap after one attempt and the harness loop was faster in 15 of 16 pairs and cheaper by a factor of three in billed tokens. The harness loop is not better on certificates: what it adds is the record, the seal, and caps that hold.
- **A smaller model is as good on these tasks.** Refuted at tier 5, where the tier-3 reading that all three models are level (18 of 18 each) turns out to be saturation: the smallest model certifies 1 of 16, the middle 13 and 14, the largest 16 of 16, and the order is strict per cell. The tier-3 result stands only where the tasks do not separate; a bench that saturates cannot rank.
- **A cheap-first cascade saves money.** Refuted on the three single-model arms ([fold](../../../../data/proving-ground/folds/2026-09-08-routing-t5-seed2.json)): because the models order strictly per cell, the oracle that picks the cheapest certifying model is the largest model alone (16 of 16 at 189,169 output tokens and 3,572 s), the strong-first cascade is the same, and every cascade that starts lower pays the smaller model's whole failure first: middle-then-large costs 2.8 times the output tokens and 2.0 times the time, cheap-first over all three 6.6 and 5.0 times. On this bench the routing question is not which model to try first but whether a hard cell can be told apart before any model runs, and nothing in the stamp tells it apart.
- **Attempts do not matter.** Refuted on tier 5 for the harness loop, first by the counterfactual (three of eleven certificates came at the second attempt after a clustered directive, delta −0.1875 for a cap of one) and then by the frozen pair: a three-rung ladder against a one-rung ladder of the same model certified 15 against 9 of 16, delta −0.375, interval [−0.5625, −0.1875], reject for the cap, the first frozen verdict about a mechanism of the harness. Half of that delta is the later attempts themselves; the other half is first-attempt variation (the two arms' first attempts, which are the same delivery, disagree on nine of sixteen cells) that the loop absorbs, so a one-attempt reading of any model on this bench is a noisier instrument than the three-attempt one. Confirmed as null on tier 3, where no second attempt was taken.
- **Knowledge helps by being present.** Refuted from both sides. A catalog the tasks do not call for is never opened and costs wall time (5,433 s against 3,959) whose cause one fleet cannot assign. Three skills the tasks do call for are opened in every cell, all three, and add no certificate: 13 of 16 against 14, 13, 15, and 15 in the four sealed runs of the same model on the same cells, folds of −0.0625, 0, −0.125, and −0.125 that never rise above zero, one task missed that no baseline had missed, and the highest cache-write of the five runs. What the middle model misses on these tasks is not what a page of craft tells it.

### What was supported

- **The largest product model beats the middle one on tier 5.** The one promotion-level verdict from a frozen pair: 16 of 16 against 13, 0.1875, [0.125, 0.25], the largest model first-attempt on every cell and cheaper on every measure. It ranks a model tier, not the harness.
- **Dropping the cheap model's transcript at the hand-off beats keeping it.** 14 of 16 against 11 on the same ladder, 0.1875, [0.0625, 0.3125], three flips all one way and all on the hardest tasks, as an offline fold of two arms run an hour apart on a shared subscription with sixteen cells each; the frozen pair on the fixed overlay is queued before anything is promoted on it.

### What stayed inconclusive, and why

Tiers 2 to 4 are saturated on certificates for all three product models under both loops, so every comparison there is inconclusive by construction and the information lives in steps, wall time, and spend. Tier 5 separates models completely and loops not at all; sixteen cells give an interval no narrower than ±0.19 for a real difference, and the noise floor is 0.0625. The bench can promote a model tier and it can refute; no frozen pair has promoted a harness change, the one harness mechanism with a promotion-level reading (the fresh child per rung) has it from an offline fold, and every harness change that landed this week (native tools, continuity, parity, the seal, the ladder) landed because a run showed a defect, not because an experiment showed a gain. The number to quote for the harness loop on the middle model is the held-out estimate: 15 of 16 with every certificate at the first attempt on eight environments nothing in the program had touched ([record](../../../../data/proving-ground/2026-09-08-bench-held-out-sonnet-all/manifest.json)), with the caveat that six of the eight are tier 2 or 3 and the two tier-5 tasks read 3 of 4.

### Spend

Before session continuity every harness-loop step on the route opened a fresh product session and rewrote its prefix: 4,778,361 cache-write tokens for sixteen tier-5 cells against 745,264 cache-read. With one product session resumed per harness session the spend is read-dominant: 2,185,841 cache-read against 317,394 cache-write for the large model's sixteen sealed cells, 8,515,603 against 738,281 for the middle model's. The product loop's spend was read-dominant from the start and its misses ran up to 3,459 s and 5.60 USD each until the budget-parity slice applied the cell's caps to a delegated attempt. The smallest model is the most expensive arm on record because a cell that fails three times rereads its growing transcript three times over: 21,275,855 cache-read tokens for sixteen cells.

### Routing, E5, and what the ladder makes possible

The attempt ladder lets an arm name a model per attempt and the transcript interface of the hand-off, and E5 ran on it as three readings. The downshift never happened: the largest model certified every cell at its first rung in both arms, so the cheap rung it was to hand down to never ran, and the experiment measures only the cost noise floor of one arm run twice. The handoff tax is real: the cheap-first ladder with the transcript kept recovered 12 of the 14 certificates the smallest model misses, cost 2.5 times the strong model alone, and lost two certificates the strong model never loses from a clean start. The drop reading is the one result in a harness mechanism's favour: the same ladder with a fresh in-process child per rung, so the strong model inherits the workspace and the directive but not the cheap model's transcript, certified 14 of 16 against 11 with the transcript kept, three flips all one way and all on the hardest tasks, 0.1875 with an interval of [0.0625, 0.3125]. It is an offline fold, because the frozen pair lost its candidate arm to a composition defect (the overlay patched an entry the base never names, which the include plugin skipped with a warning nothing printed) and its two arms ran an hour apart; it is evidence for the slate's prediction that dropping the cheap transcript beats keeping it, not a promotion, and the frozen pair is queued. Where the fresh child still missed, the cheap rung alone had spent the cell's token cap before the strong rung ran, which is a budget allocation defect of the ladder, not a transcript effect. On these cells raw escalation still cannot beat starting strong on cost, so what the ladder buys is a cheaper answer on the cells the cheap model can do (2 of 16 here) at a higher total cost on the rest. A per-turn router in the style of Fugu is not tested by any of this and is not proposed.

## Slices the results demand

- **Interleave the arms.** The experiments service schedules cells pairwise (baseline then candidate of the same cell, alternating) so an arm is not confounded with its hour. Small; the digest and the fold are unchanged.
- **Freeze the drop pair and share the cap per rung.** The drop comparison runs again as one frozen experiment on the fixed overlay, and each rung of a ladder gets its own share of the cell's token cap, so a cheap first rung cannot spend the strong rung's budget; the two misses of the drop arm were exactly that.
- **A harder tier or more cells before any harness promotion.** Sixteen cells cannot show a harness effect smaller than 0.19; the next paired harness experiment runs four repetitions or sixteen tasks, and the tier-5 tasks that every model certifies at the first attempt (five of eight for the middle model) do not discriminate.
- **Program workflow integrity** ([note](2026-09-08-program-workflow-integrity.md)): a department certifies only a committed tree, the integration runs confined, an interrupted integration resumes.
- **The process claim over the route**: the census attributes the route's own product spawn to the route so a route cell can claim `process` isolation (task 85).

## Alternatives considered

- **Report the certificate rates as a leaderboard.** Rejected: the observatory ranks only from experiment verdicts; the two promotion-level verdicts on record rank model tiers, and a leaderboard of the rest would rank noise.
- **Raise the repetitions until an interval excludes zero.** Rejected for the loop comparison: at two cells at a time a tier-5 experiment of 32 cells takes two and a half hours, and the reading that the two loops are level on tiers 2 to 4 would not change with more cells; the money goes to tier 5, the ladder, and a harder tier.
- **Import a public benchmark before drawing any conclusion.** Deferred as the slate says; the readings above are about this harness on this bench, and say so.

## Acceptance criteria

- Every row of the table above points at a record or fold under `data/proving-ground/` with sessions on disk.
- The results section of the slate note links here, and the observatory shows no ranking from these runs beyond the two model-tier verdicts.
- No row of the table is a placeholder: the sealed E2 rerun, the frozen E3 pair, the craft fold, the three E5 readings, and the held-out estimate each replaced theirs in the same change as their record.

## Risks

- Sixteen cells per arm on tier 5 is a small sample; the counterfactual intervals understate run-to-run variance and are labelled so; the noise floor above is measured from two runs, not twenty.
- The drop reading is an offline fold of two arms run an hour apart, and its candidate arm ran only after the frozen pair failed; its verdict label comes from the fold's own thresholds, and this note treats it as evidence.
- The arms ran sequentially on a shared subscription; a model-side change or load between the two arms would read as an arm effect.
- The tasks are authored in-house; the audit fixed three specification corners after both loops missed them, and a later audit may find more.
- The product envelope around every route query is model-visible and not in the log; both arms carry it, so comparisons hold and absolute costs include it.
