### MemoryWalker: Stop Training Agents on Contexts They Never Saw

- Date 2026-09-01 · kind paper · role input · evidence primary · goals 3 · seams compaction, compaction-basic, compaction-tool-result-pruner, session-projection, trajectories
- URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00865>
- Summary: Submitted 1 September 2026. Production harnesses including Claude Code and Qwen-Agent compress context during rollout, so training under compression creates a conditioning problem: every eviction branches the effective history and the learning object becomes a tree rather than a sequence. The paper gives three treatments — LogitTree (K+1 backward passes with segmented computation), a 4D attention mask requiring custom kernels and white-box eviction records, and SDCC, a single-backward-pass self-distillation minimising KL between compressed and uncompressed versions with an O(sqrt(eps_KL)) bound on the train–deployment total-variation gap. Evaluation spans TC-RAG, AgentFold, MemexRL, Claude Code and OpenCode across seven web-search benchmarks; exact methods hold the no-compression baseline and SDCC substantially closes the gap with lower logit drift and higher rollout rewards.
- Adopt: This is the sharpest constraint on goal 3 in the window: if we train on trajectories our harness compacted, we train on contexts the model never saw. Our compaction is already a logged plugin with a reconstructable projection, which is precisely the white-box eviction record the exact methods need — that is an advantage over closed harnesses and we should say so. Start by exporting eviction points as first-class events, then evaluate SDCC before building anything exotic.
- Tags: compaction, context-management, rlvr, training-deployment-gap

### Uncovering and Mitigating Aggregation-Induced Reward Hacking in Multi-Reward Reinforcement Learning

- Date 2026-08-31 · kind paper · role input · evidence primary · goals 3 · seams curator, judge, scorekeeper, verification
- URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00213>
- Summary: Submitted 31 August 2026. The paper shows that aggregation itself induces reward hacking: static projection of multiple reward dimensions into one scalar aliases qualitatively different reward profiles, steering optimisation toward whichever dimensions are easiest, densest or systematically favoured. Adaptive Multi-Reward Projection reallocates weights using relative shortfall, reward volatility and recent progress, raising pressure on underperforming, unstable or stagnant dimensions and lowering it on saturated ones. It reports improved reward-profile balance and downstream performance under GRPO, and holds under GDPO and PPO.
- Adopt: Our certified runs will produce several verifiable signals — tests, certificate satisfaction, tamper verdict, budget adherence — and the naive move is one weighted sum. Keep the reward profile as a vector in the session log and make the projection an explicit, configurable, logged step, so a shift can show which dimension the policy actually optimised.

### Harness-RL: black-box RL with action-args decoupling for central-agent multi-agent harnesses

- Date 2026-08-30 · kind paper · role input · evidence primary · goals 3 · seams curator, subagent, tool-subagent, trajectories
- URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29641>
- Summary: Submitted 30 August 2026. Harness-RL trains the central policy of a harness in which one agent coordinates sub-agents, tools and environments, addressing two problems: an action label is a low-cardinality decision while its arguments are a high-dimensional conditional sequence, so a shared sequence-level signal produces conflicting gradients; and dynamic scheduling creates interdependent sessions with branches, parallel calls and rewritten contexts that cannot be flattened into one token sequence. It captures Interface Call Records, builds per-session prefix trees, aligns outcome and process rewards with trainable tokens, and routes action and args gradients into separate parameter subspaces (Conflict-Aware Policy Optimization). Reported average F1 across seven multi-hop QA and agentic retrieval benchmarks is 42.93 with Qwen2.5-1.5B and 47.79 with Qwen2.5-3B.
- Adopt: The structural claim matters more than the F1 numbers at 1.5–3B: a harness session with parallel subagents and rewritten context is a tree, and our trajectory export currently serialises it. Record branch structure and interface call boundaries in the export now, while it is cheap, or every future training run pays to reconstruct them. Their Interface Call Record is close enough to our session event envelope to be worth a direct comparison.
- Tags: credit-assignment, multi-agent, rl, trajectory-structure
