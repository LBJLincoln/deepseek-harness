# Hugging Face sweep, 1 June - 7 September 2026

Source: Hugging Face Hub (models, datasets, Spaces, daily papers, docs), read through the Hub MCP connector; release dates for TRL, PEFT and vLLM cross-read from their public release pages. Everything below is dated from repository metadata or the model/dataset card itself unless marked otherwise.

## 1. The ~120B base-model decision

The window did not produce a new 100-130B open-weight coding model. It produced something more useful: **proof that the 119B Apache-2.0 checkpoint Daliesk would build on is already being fine-tuned and redistributed by its own author.**

[`mistralai/Leanstral-1.5-119B-A6B`](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Leanstral-1.5-119B-A6B) was created on **1 July 2026** under **Apache-2.0**. Its card states 119B total parameters with 6.5B activated per token, 128 experts with 4 active, 256k context, multimodal input, and describes it as "an open-source code agent model" in the Mistral Small 4 family, derived from `mistralai/Leanstral-2603`. Within a week, community derivatives appeared under Apache-2.0 in GGUF, MLX 3/4/5/6/8-bit, NVFP4 and MXFP4-W4A8 form (created 4-8 July 2026). The parent, [`mistralai/Mistral-Small-4-119B-2603`](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-Small-4-119B-2603) (created 23 January 2026, Apache-2.0, "119B parameters, with 6.5B activated per token", 256k context), states in its own card that it is "well-suited for customization and fine-tuning" and points at Axolotl for that. Its NVFP4 checkpoint was touched as recently as **7 September 2026**. For a European lab that must own the redistribution rights of a ~120B model, this is the only clean answer on the Hub: European provenance, Apache-2.0 on both base and derivative, 6.5B active parameters, and a worked example of the exact fine-tune-and-republish move.

The alternative in the same size class is not redistributable on the same terms. [`nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B-BF16`](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B-BF16) is 120B total / 12B active, up to 1M context, with a card release date of 11 March 2026 and repository updates on 24-25 August 2026. It is genuinely open in the ways that matter for the EU AI Act - pre-training and post-training datasets published, an end-to-end recipe, RL environments released through NeMo Gym, evaluation reproducible via Nemo Evaluator - and it reports SWE-Bench (OpenHands) 60.47, Terminal Bench Core 2.0 31.00 and Terminal Bench hard-subset 25.78. But it ships under the bespoke NVIDIA Nemotron Open Model License, not Apache-2.0.

The most instructive NVIDIA release inside the window is the compression, not the model. [`nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-Labs-3-Puzzle-75B-A9B-BF16`](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-Labs-3-Puzzle-75B-A9B-BF16), released **6 July 2026** under **OpenMDW-1.1**, takes the 120.7B/12.8B parent down to 75.3B/9.3B through Iterative Puzzle compression plus knowledge distillation, RL recovery, quantization and an MTP head. The card reports 24B + 43.2B + 52.8B tokens of distillation recovery across three stages, roughly 2x server throughput on one 8xB200 node at matched user-throughput, sustainable 1M-token single-H100 concurrency rising from 1 request to 8, and SWE-Bench (OpenHands) 56.9 against the parent's 59.5. That is a published, licensed answer to "how do we serve a 120B-class agent on modest hardware, and what does it cost in SWE-bench points".

Licensing moved the wrong way at the top end. [`zai-org/GLM-5.2`](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.2) was created **16 June 2026** under **MIT**. Two months later [`zai-org/GLM-5.3`](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.3) (created **25 August 2026**, same base model, "every gain comes from post-training") shipped under a bespoke `glm-5.3` licence, while its sibling [`zai-org/GLM-5.3-Flash`](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.3-Flash) (also 25 August 2026, 321B) stayed MIT. Read that as a warning: permissive licensing at the frontier is now conditional and can be withdrawn version-to-version. GLM-5.3's card reports Terminal Bench 3.0 28.3 (against 4.6 for GLM-5.2) and DeepSWE v1.1 66.9.

## 2. Small MoE coding models that read as recipes, not products

Three sub-40B releases in the window matter more for what they document than for their weights.

[`Kwaipilot/KAT-Coder-V2.5-Dev`](https://huggingface.co/Kwaipilot/KAT-Coder-V2.5-Dev), created **23 July 2026** under **Apache-2.0**, is 35B total / 3B active on a Qwen3.6-35B-A3B base. It reports SWE-bench Verified 69.40, SWE-bench Multilingual 63.00, SWE-bench Pro 45.96, Terminal-Bench 2.1 41.02 (32.60 Terminus-2 / 49.44 Claude Code) and SciCode 44.20, all reproduced in-house at 256k context. The post-training section is the payload: Token-in-Token-out consistency so rollout and training token sequences are identical, Truncated Importance Sampling for asynchronous-rollout staleness, explicit validation that sandboxes and verifiers are correct so infrastructure failures are not scored as model failures, and **hierarchical rewards built from the harness's own execution feedback**. It also records a concrete failure: a binary 0-1 reward collapsed the model by epoch two, with the policy emitting occasionally more than 70 parallel tool calls in one turn, fixed only by adding penalties for excessive parallel calls, failed calls, empty tool-call blocks and repeated content.

[`ornith-ai/Ornith-1.5-35B-A3B`](https://huggingface.co/ornith-ai/Ornith-1.5-35B-A3B) (MIT; quantized siblings created **18 August 2026**) is the direct competitor to Daliesk's fourth goal. Its card describes expanding a self-improvement loop "from scaffold and rollout optimization to jointly optimizing task generation, scaffold construction, and solution rollouts", explicitly replacing "a fixed set of human-curated tasks and manually designed harnesses". It reports Terminal-Bench 2.1 of 67.8 (Terminus-2) and 68.5 (Claude Code), SWE-bench Verified 79 and SWE-bench Pro 59.6 at 3B active parameters. Someone has already built the harness auto-improvement loop and shipped the model under MIT.

[`IFM/K2-Horizon-MoVA-36B-A4B`](https://huggingface.co/IFM/K2-Horizon-MoVA-36B-A4B) (family created **1-3 September 2026**, Apache-2.0) is 36B/4B with 512K native context, and promises intermediate checkpoints, training data and training code. Its pre-training corpus is already partly public as [`IFM/TxT360-v2`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/IFM/TxT360-v2) (CC-BY-4.0, updated 3 September 2026), alongside Code-Reasoning and Math-Reasoning repositories. This is the cleanest published template for the training-data summary an EU general-purpose-model filing needs.

Finally, [`Qwen/Qwen-AgentWorld-35B-A3B`](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-AgentWorld-35B-A3B) (created **22 June 2026**, Apache-2.0, 35B/3B, 262,144-token context) is a *language world model* that simulates seven agent domains - MCP, Search, Terminal, SWE, Android, Web, OS - predicting the next environment state given an action and history, trained CPT -> SFT -> RL(GSPO). Its card claims zero-shot generalization to out-of-distribution environments and controllable perturbations. For an environments registry that must scale past the environments someone hand-authored, a licensed simulator is a serious option.

## 3. Verifier-bearing data and environments

The Hub's RLVR-for-software corpus consolidated around Harbor and Prime Intellect during the window.

Harbor decouples a **task** (instruction + sandbox image + verifier) from a **harness/agent** (tool surface + loop) from a **sandbox** (docker, e2b, daytona, gke) - the phrasing is from [TRL's Harbor integration doc](https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/harbor). Its Hub mirrors moved in-window: [`terminal-bench-3.0`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/harborframework/terminal-bench-3.0) and `terminal-bench-2.1` on 21 August 2026, [`terminal-bench-science`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/harborframework/terminal-bench-science) on 3 September 2026, all Apache-2.0, laid out for Harbor's `git-repos` dataset support.

Two datasets are evidence that public verifiers are unreliable. [`zai-org/terminal-bench-2-verified`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/zai-org/terminal-bench-2-verified) (24 August 2026, Apache-2.0) fixes six Terminal-Bench 2.0 tasks whose "test logic itself misjudged valid submissions", each fix validated end-to-end inside the real task images. [`PrimeIntellect/Multi-SWE-RL-Verified`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PrimeIntellect/Multi-SWE-RL-Verified) (8 July 2026) keeps only 2,232 of 4,703 rows that "produce a clean reward signal end-to-end", and drops C++ wholesale because 0 of 449 rows passed gold-patch validation. A reward that is not gold-patch-validated is a reward you do not have.

Volume is available. [`nebius/SWE-rebench-V2-PRs`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nebius/SWE-rebench-V2-PRs) (18 August 2026, CC-BY-4.0) holds 126,300 real GitHub pull requests across 17+ languages; [`PrimeIntellect/SWE-rebench-V2-Filtered-Verified`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PrimeIntellect/SWE-rebench-V2-Filtered-Verified) (20 July 2026, CC-BY-4.0) is the 6,272-of-32,079 gold-patch-verified cut; [`PrimeIntellect/Terminal-Lego-15k`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PrimeIntellect/Terminal-Lego-15k) (22 July 2026, Apache-2.0) is 13,825 terminal task directories each with `instruction.md`, `task.toml`, an environment Dockerfile and tests, with prebuilt images.

Two items speak straight to Daliesk's tamper-verdict seam. [`IntelligenceLab/Long-Horizon-Terminal-Bench`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/IntelligenceLab/Long-Horizon-Terminal-Bench) (19 August 2026, Apache-2.0, 46 tasks) grades "with hidden, rebuild-from-artifact verifiers - self-reported progress does not count". And [`PrimeIntellect/trace-cheating-recall-500`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PrimeIntellect/trace-cheating-recall-500) (4 September 2026) is 500 SWE-agent traces, all labelled CHEATING - 250 internet-retrieval, 250 Git-history - published as the data source for a Prime environment that measures whether an LLM judge actually detects observable solution leakage. That is a recall test for a tamper verdict, ready to run.

NVIDIA published [`NeMo-Gym-EnterpriseOps-Assets`](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/NeMo-Gym-EnterpriseOps-Assets) on 30 August 2026 (Apache-2.0): seven per-domain `tools/list` schema snapshots captured from public MCP gym containers, too large to commit to the NeMo Gym repo.

## 4. Tooling with in-window releases

**TRL v1.10.0 (13 August 2026)** graduated `DistillationTrainer` to stable and added AsyncGRPO "loop-owning agent training" - a black-box path for external agents via the OpenEnv harness. **TRL v1.11.0 (26 August 2026)** replaced TRL's own FastAPI generation server with vLLM's native server, cutting ~1218 lines to ~130 and reporting a 1.59x GRPO speedup, and added an experimental `AsyncDistillationTrainer` for multi-teacher on-policy distillation. (v1.12.0, same day, is documented as bit-identical to v1.11.0.) The architectural point for Daliesk is the single `environment_factory` slot: **OpenEnv, OpenReward and Harbor are interchangeable at the TRL level**, and TRL's own docs are explicit that only *external* agents can be trained - an agent installed inside the container runs its own model and emits a trajectory after the fact, so "there are no policy tokens or log-probs for the trainer to optimize". A harness that wants to be RL-trainable must be drivable turn-by-turn from outside.

**PEFT v0.20.0 (28 July 2026)** added nine methods (HiRA, GLoRA, BEFT, MonteCLoRA, VeLoRA, Uni-LoRA, FRoD, MiCA, DEFT), automatic LoRA target selection via KappaTuneSelector, and broader quantization coverage. **vLLM v0.27.0 / v0.27.1 (10-11 August 2026) and v0.28.0 (26 August 2026)** made Model Runner V2 the standard path, removed PagedAttention, and added FlashAttention 4, KV offloading and dynamic speculative decoding compatible with full CUDA graphs - dates from changelog aggregators, so secondary.

## 5. Daily papers in the window

Listed briefly; the arXiv researcher covers these. Directly load-bearing: **Terminal-Universe** ([2609.04148](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04148), 3 Sep, 273 upvotes) reconstructs executable workspaces *from agent trajectories* to synthesize training tasks - Daliesk's trajectory-export idea, published. **LEGO-RL** ([2608.17393](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.17393), 18 Aug) connects native coding-agent harnesses to policy-gradient training through in-process LLM proxying and sandbox orchestration, improving sparse MoE models across multiple harnesses. **Apodex 1.1** ([2608.23283](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.23283), 24 Aug, 205 upvotes) scales executable environments for long-horizon work with state maintenance and recovery. **Prime Agent** ([2608.23552](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.23552), 24 Aug) is an open-source self-improving harness with recursive subagents and agent-to-agent coordination.

On rewards: **The Verification Horizon** ([2606.26300](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.26300), 24 Jun, 53 upvotes) argues no single proxy signal aligns with intent and verification must co-evolve; **CHERRL** ([2606.04923](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.04923), 3 Jun) reproduces and detects reward hacking in rubric-based RL; **RobustTests** ([2608.24135](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.24135), 25 Aug) synthesizes test cases from faulty code to reduce reward hacking; **DRACO** ([2609.04094](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04094), 3 Sep) redistributes trajectory scores into per-step advantages with dynamic rubrics; **Locked at the Entrance, Open Inside** ([2608.29188](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.29188), 29 Aug) finds RLVR narrows diversity at the *first* step, not during execution. On evaluation: **ClawProBench** ([2608.22510](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.22510), 23 Aug) evaluates agent configurations by execution trace across live and frozen holdout tracks; **Thinkingbox** ([2608.19741](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.19741), 20 Aug, Microsoft) reports a best model at 65.36% pass@1 but 25.25% pass^20 on stateful business workflows; **Task-CoEvolve** ([2608.20169](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.20169), 24 Aug) cuts harness-optimization evaluation cost by 80% through adaptive validation-task selection. Also relevant: **Environment Evolution for Terminal Agents** ([2609.04128](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.04128)), **RealSWE** ([2608.27831](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.27831)), **Grounded Theory for Agent Behavior Analysis** ([2608.30391](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.30391)).

## 6. Competitors to the fleet idea

The Hub now hosts the fleet pattern natively. [`ICML-2026-agent-repro/challenge`](https://huggingface.co/spaces/ICML-2026-agent-repro/challenge) (ran 15 July - 2 August 2026, 218 likes) ran a community agent fleet reproducing the major claims of every ICML 2026 paper: one Trackio logbook per paper, "a Hub-native record ... readable by humans and by coding agents", per-claim progress rolled up across the conference, organiser-verified verdicts, certificates of participation, a `certificate-generator` Space, a `logbook-judge` Space, and $4,000 in HF GPU credits. [`agent-collaborations/agent-collab-directory`](https://huggingface.co/spaces/agent-collaborations/agent-collab-directory) (23 June 2026) auto-discovers every Space tagged `agent-collab` and reads live agent counts, message counts and submissions from each collaboration's public API.

[`smolagents/ml-intern`](https://huggingface.co/spaces/smolagents/ml-intern) (18 June 2026, 418 likes) is Hugging Face's own autonomous ML agent CLI. Every session auto-uploads to the operator's **own private** HF dataset in Claude Code JSONL format, auto-detected by the Hub's Agent Trace Viewer (a Hub feature announced 7 April 2026, supporting Claude Code, Codex, Pi, Hermes Agent and Factory Droid traces), with `/share-traces public|private` and an opt-out. That is trajectory export with data-use terms, shipped, by the platform.

Two leaderboards bracket the harness-measurement problem. [`red-hat-emerging-technologies/coding-agent-leaderboard`](https://huggingface.co/spaces/red-hat-emerging-technologies/coding-agent-leaderboard) (created 2 September 2026) compares coding-agent **models x harnesses** per benchmark on score against total tokens, cost per task and agent time per task, with an explicit Pareto frontier and entries submitted as PRs to a `results/` folder. [`hlido-eu/leaderboard`](https://huggingface.co/spaces/hlido-eu/leaderboard) (updated 6 September 2026) scores *shipped agent products* from the outside against their public claims, with **C2PA cryptographically signed evidence** - "tamper-evident proof the test ran as described" - longitudinal re-tests, an MCP server and a public registry. A European outfit is already selling signed agent certificates.

## What Daliesk should do

1. **Fix the base model on `mistralai/Mistral-Small-4-119B-2603` (Apache-2.0, 119B/6.5B active, 256k)** and treat `Leanstral-1.5-119B-A6B` as the licensing and packaging precedent. Goal 3; touches the model-training track and `packages/llm`'s provider seam for serving the result.
2. **Budget a compression stage now, not later.** Reproduce the Puzzle-75B-A9B ratio target (120B/12B to 75B/9B for ~2.6 SWE-bench points) as an explicit line in the goal-3 plan; it decides the fleet's GPU bill. Goal 3, and the budget-policy seam in goal 4.
3. **Adopt KAT-Coder-V2.5-Dev's reward design wholesale as the starting point**: hierarchical rewards from harness execution feedback, TITO, truncated importance sampling, and named penalties for parallel-tool-call explosions. Goal 3; touches `packages/core/agent-loop` telemetry and the session event log, since every reward term must be reconstructable from logged events.
4. **Make the environment runner conform to Harbor's decomposition** - task (instruction + sandbox image + verifier) / harness / sandbox - so the environments registry can consume `terminal-bench-3.0`, `Terminal-Lego-15k` and Harbor task trees without a bespoke adapter. Goals 2 and 4; environments registry and environment runner.
5. **Keep the harness externally drivable turn-by-turn.** TRL's Harbor doc is explicit that installed agents cannot be RL-trained because the trainer sees no policy tokens or log-probs. Daliesk's subagent seam and in-process MCP tool bridge must expose a rollout path where the policy under training generates each turn. Goals 1 and 3.
6. **Run `trace-cheating-recall-500` against the tamper verdict before trusting it.** 500 known-cheating SWE traces is a recall floor; publish the number. Goal 3; tamper verdicts on the reward, and the curator's redaction profiles.
7. **Gold-patch-validate every imported environment and publish the survival rate.** Multi-SWE-RL lost 53% of rows and all of C++; `terminal-bench-2-verified` found six mis-grading tasks. Make "verifier validated on a restored fixture" a certificate precondition. Goal 4; environment runner and certificates.
8. **Treat Ornith-1.5 and Terminal-Universe as the competitive baseline for goal 4, not as inspiration.** Both publish the loop Daliesk is building; the defensible difference is the certified-run provenance and the held-out environments, so make those the measured claim. Goals 3 and 4; composition manifest and sign-off records.
9. **Ship trajectory export in the Claude Code JSONL format the Hub's trace viewer auto-detects, private by default with an explicit publish action.** `ml-intern` has set the operator expectation. Goal 3; trajectory export and data-use terms pinned to sessions.
10. **Publish a training-data summary in the K2-Horizon / Nemotron style before the seed round.** Both families disclose pre-training and post-training corpora as Hub datasets with licences; that is the EU AI Act general-purpose-model documentation obligation, already demonstrated. Goal 3; also the client-facing story for the five European listed companies.
