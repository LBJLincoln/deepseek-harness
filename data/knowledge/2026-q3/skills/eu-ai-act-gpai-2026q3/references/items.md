### ruflo verification/: signed per-OS witness manifests

- Date 2026-09-05 · kind policy · role input · evidence primary · goals 2, 4 · seams ctx.completionStandards, ctx.components content addressing, packages/bundle
- URL: <https://github.com/ruvnet/ruflo/blob/main/verification/README.md>
- Summary: A cross-platform regression-protection toolkit, not a task verifier. Every documented fix is attested by a SHA-256 file hash, a marker substring documented as proof the fix exists, and an Ed25519 signature that anyone on the same commit can re-derive and verify. Manifests are per-OS (linux/macos/windows) because file hashes drift across platforms, each with its own append-only `history.jsonl` for bisecting when a regression appeared, and a `witness-verify` CI job that blocks publish when a marker disappears. It exists because three regressions filed on 8 May 2026 passed unit tests on the broken commits but broke users on first install. Statuses distinguish exact match, semantic match after drift, and source-only match when the dist is unbuilt.
- Adopt: The signed, re-derivable, per-OS attestation is genuinely better than anything we ship for released artefacts, and it costs one dependency. Adopt the mechanism for our composition manifest: a signed content address for each mounted component per platform, verified in CI before publish. Reject the marker-substring heuristic itself — a substring is not a behavioural check, and our completion standards already execute real ones.
- Tags: attestation, ci, ed25519, regression, supply-chain

### Mapping General-Purpose AI Governance in Twenty AI Middle-Power Jurisdictions

- Date 2026-08-18 · kind policy · role input · evidence primary · goals 3 · seams composition-manifest, settings, sign-off-records
- URL: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19278>
- Summary: Arcadia Impact's AI Governance Taskforce with The Future Society map GPAI-relevant provisions at the individual-provision level across twenty jurisdictions including the EU, over four governance areas (systemic risk assessment, evaluation and verification, prohibitions with breach monitoring, serious incident reporting), recording confirmed absences as data: 101 instruments, 587 provision records (382 positive, 205 absences). Only 22% of provisions sit in binding law; ten of twenty jurisdictions hold no binding in-force GPAI provision; the EU (26) and Brazil (18) hold more than half of the 85 binding provisions; three quarters of binding instruments impose obligations without defining GPAI at all; 58% of instruments regulate GPAI without defining it. Outside the EU, no jurisdiction imposes a binding evaluation duty on a model developer, and self-assessment is the majority evaluation model in 11 of 17 jurisdictions. Compute thresholds do not align: the EU AI Act presumes systemic risk above 1e25 FLOP, rebuttable under Article 52, while Korea's 2026 Enforcement Decree sets 1e26 as one of three cumulative conditions, so a model trained between them is in scope in the EU and out of scope in Korea. Provision dataset deposited on Zenodo under CC-BY-4.0.
- Adopt: Adopt as the reference for which duties actually bind per client jurisdiction, and cite the provision dataset rather than secondary summaries. The operational conclusion for a ~120B model: both compute thresholds are far above it, so the binding constraints are the EU's documentation, transparency and training-data-summary duties, not the systemic-risk tier. Reject the assumption that non-EU markets impose comparable developer-side duties - they mostly do not.
- Tags: comparative-law, compute-threshold, dataset, eu-ai-act, governance, gpai

### MCP specification revision 2026-07-28 (stateless core)

- Date 2026-07-28 · kind policy · role input · evidence primary · goals 1, 2 · seams ctx.tools, interaction/approval seam, packages/mcp (both directions)
- URL: <https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28/changelog>
- Summary: The largest MCP revision since launch. The initialize/notifications-initialized handshake and Mcp-Session-Id are removed; protocol version, client info and capabilities ride in per-request `_meta`; a mandatory `server/discover` RPC replaces the handshake; every result carries `resultType` (`complete` / `input_required`); list results carry `ttlMs` and `cacheScope`; Streamable HTTP POSTs must carry `Mcp-Method`/`Mcp-Name` headers validated against the body; `subscriptions/listen` replaces the GET SSE channel and `resources/subscribe`; MRTR (multi round-trip requests) replaces server-initiated `sampling/createMessage`, `elicitation/create` and `roots/list`; tasks move to a redesigned official extension; sampling, roots and MCP logging are deprecated on a 12-month window; tool schemas loosen to full JSON Schema 2020-12; dynamic client registration is deprecated in favour of CIMD. Implementers are shipping dual-era servers rather than cutting over.
- Adopt: Plan a dual-era MCP server: keep the 2025-11-25 path and add a 2026-07-28 path with `server/discover`, `resultType`, and `ttlMs`/`cacheScope` on `tools/list`. Reject the temptation to model MCP sessions as harness sessions — statelessness means any cross-call state becomes an explicit server-minted handle passed as a tool argument, which is closer to how our session log already works. Our approval seam should treat MRTR as the replacement path for elicitation before the deprecation window closes.
- Tags: breaking-change, mcp, protocol, tool-bridge

### ruflo ADR-169: benchmark reporting integrity standard

- Date 2026-07-03 · kind policy · role input · evidence primary · goals 3, 4 · seams ctx.experiments, ctx.observatory, ctx.scorekeeper, ctx.trajectories
- URL: <https://github.com/ruvnet/ruflo/blob/main/v3/docs/adr/ADR-169-benchmark-reporting-integrity-standard.md>
- Summary: A binding in-repository standard requiring every benchmark number ruflo publishes — README, release notes, leaderboard submission, gist or blog — to satisfy five rules, motivated by an April 2026 Berkeley RDI study that broke eight major benchmarks through reporting artefacts that inflate scores with no cheating in the run: relaxed or substring metrics presented as accuracy, undisclosed best-of-N presented as single-attempt, no-work passes hidden in aggregates, and unreproducible cherry-picked runs. The ADR was written after a FRAMES ablation (n=50, seed 42, 28 June 2026) demonstrated the discipline is cheap to satisfy by construction. It stands beside ADR-171, which states plainly that 'ruflo has no SWE-bench oracle' and requires every training label to be tagged with its provenance tier rather than blended.
- Adopt: Adopt both, adapted to our vocabulary. ADR-169's five rules belong in the observatory's render path so an unlabelled or best-of-N figure cannot be published. ADR-171's provenance tiers map onto our reward: a certificate-decided reward is ground truth, a judge verdict is a labelled second tier, and nothing else may be blended into a single number. Neither exists in our repository today.
- Tags: adr, benchmark-integrity, provenance, reward-labels

### Gemini CLI stops serving individual accounts; Antigravity CLI replaces it

- Date 2026-06-18 · kind policy · role baseline · evidence primary · goals 1 · seams ctx.llm provider routes, ctx.subagents, packages/credentials
- URL: <https://developers.googleblog.com/an-important-update-transitioning-gemini-cli-to-antigravity-cli/>
- Summary: Google announced that on 18 June 2026 Gemini CLI and the Gemini Code Assist IDE extensions stop serving requests for Google AI Pro, Ultra and free individual tiers, which move to Antigravity CLI (`agy`), a closed-source Go binary. Enterprise access through Gemini Code Assist Standard/Enterprise licences and paid API keys is unchanged. Secondary reports describe the retired repository as Apache-2.0 with roughly 104k stars and about 6,000 external contributors, and Antigravity CLI as cloud-required and Gemini-only with per-conversation history in SQLite under `~/.gemini/antigravity-cli/`.
- Adopt: This is the strongest argument in the window for our model-agnostic thesis: a 104k-star open harness with a vendor behind it can stop serving requests on a date. Reject any design that assumes an operator's vendor CLI stays available; the `llm-claude-code` and `subagent-codex` routes must remain one provider among several, never a load-bearing default. Worth adding an Antigravity/`agy` subagent provider only if a client asks.
- Tags: antigravity, gemini-cli, shutdown, vendor-risk
