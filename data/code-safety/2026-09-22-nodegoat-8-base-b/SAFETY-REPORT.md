# Code-safety review — OWASP NodeGoat

## Résumé exécutif

Cette revue de sécurité porte sur OWASP NodeGoat (`/root/targets/NodeGoat`), une application Express/MongoDB volontairement vulnérable, verrouillée à 111 fichiers par leur hachage SHA-256 dans `target.json`. Six départements ont chacun lu tout ou partie de cet arbre : accès (authentification, session, autorisation), données (exposition de données sensibles, journalisation, cryptographie), dépendances (manifeste et verrou npm), injection (SQL/NoSQL, code, XSS, SSRF, ReDoS), plateforme (en-têtes, cookies, configuration) et secrets (identifiants et clés codés en dur). Après déduplication par fichier, ligne et CWE, l'union tient 46 constats : 10 critiques, 18 élevés, 14 moyens, 3 faibles et 1 informatif. Les plus importants : (1) trois injections de code côté serveur via `eval()` sur les champs de cotisation de retraite (`app/routes/contributions.js:32-34`), qui permettent à tout utilisateur authentifié d'exécuter du JavaScript arbitraire sur le serveur ; (2) le secret de signature des cookies de session codé en dur (`config/env/all.js:8`) et jamais remplacé en production, qui permet de forger un cookie de session pour n'importe quel utilisateur ; (3) un mot de passe administrateur par défaut en clair, inséré par le script de seed exécuté à chaque déploiement (`artifacts/db-reset.js:18`), qui donne un accès administrateur immédiat. La revue a porté sur les routes HTTP, les DAO MongoDB, la configuration, les vues rendues côté serveur et le manifeste de dépendances ; elle n'a couvert ni l'historique Git, ni l'exécution réelle de l'application, ni les tests dynamiques, ni les bibliothèques front-end embarquées, ni la chaîne d'approvisionnement au-delà des avis npm connus (voir « Ce qui n'a pas été couvert »). Méthode : lecture des points d'entrée avant toute chose, suivi des données de la source jusqu'au point d'exécution ou de sortie, un constat par point d'exécution, ligne citée exactement (vérifiée mécaniquement par `verify-safety-report.mjs` contre le texte réel de l'arbre verrouillé), et niveau de confiance déclaré pour chaque constat ; l'analyse est celle d'un modèle de langage, étayée par l'outil `semgrep` (utilisé comme point de départ de lecture, jamais comme preuve à lui seul) et par `npm audit`/`npm view` pour les dépendances, puis vérifiée mécaniquement par l'examinateur commis `verify-safety-report.mjs`.

## Executive summary

This review covers OWASP NodeGoat (`/root/targets/NodeGoat`), a deliberately vulnerable Express/MongoDB application, locked to 111 files by the SHA-256 recorded in `target.json`. Six departments each read all or part of this tree: access (authentication, session, authorization), data (sensitive data exposure, logging, cryptography), dependencies (npm manifest and lockfile), injection (SQL/NoSQL, code, XSS, SSRF, ReDoS), platform (headers, cookies, configuration) and secrets (hard-coded credentials and keys). After deduplication by file, line and CWE, the union holds 46 findings: 10 critical, 18 high, 14 medium, 3 low and 1 info. The most important: (1) three server-side code-injection sinks via `eval()` on retirement-contribution fields (`app/routes/contributions.js:32-34`), letting any authenticated user run arbitrary JavaScript on the server; (2) the session-cookie signing secret is hard-coded (`config/env/all.js:8`) and never overridden in production, letting anyone forge a session cookie for any user; (3) a default plaintext admin password is inserted by the seed script that runs on every deploy (`artifacts/db-reset.js:18`), giving immediate administrative access. The review covered HTTP routes, MongoDB DAOs, configuration, server-rendered views, and the dependency manifest; it did not cover git history, running the application, dynamic testing, bundled front-end libraries, or supply-chain provenance beyond known npm advisories (see "What was not covered"). Method: read entry points first, trace data from source to sink or exit, one finding per sink, exact line citations (mechanically checked by `verify-safety-report.mjs` against the locked tree's own text), and a stated confidence level per finding; this is a language-model review grounded by the `semgrep` scanner (used to steer reading, never as evidence on its own) and by `npm audit`/`npm view` for dependencies, then mechanically checked by the committed examiner `verify-safety-report.mjs`.

## Scope and method

Target: `/root/targets/NodeGoat`, locked to 111 files by the SHA-256 recorded in `target.json` (`.git` excluded), read-only for every department and for this integration.

Six departments ran against this tree and each committed `findings/<department>.json` plus `report/<department>.md` on its own branch, merged into this worktree: **access** (authentication, session management, authorization/IDOR, CSRF), **data** (sensitive data exposure, logging, cryptography), **dependencies** (manifest and lockfile analysis), **injection** (SQL/NoSQL, code injection, XSS, SSRF, open redirect, ReDoS), **platform** (security headers, cookies, TLS, general misconfiguration), and **secrets** (hard-coded credentials, keys and connection strings). This document is a review by a language model, grounded by a static scanner and by the committed mechanical examiner (`verify-safety-report.mjs`), not an automated or exhaustive audit.

Tools that ran: `semgrep 1.177.0` (the access, data, injection, platform and secrets departments each ran it from the target root with a local rule pack plus the registry pack `p/owasp-top-ten`, registry reachable, 113 rules over 72 git-tracked files, 29 raw hits per run) — every hit was read in context and used only to decide what to open next, never reported as a finding on the strength of the pattern match alone. The dependencies department instead ran `npm audit --json` and `npm view <pkg> time deprecated version --json` against the reachable npm registry over `package.json`/`package-lock.json` (1,479 resolved packages; 145 advisories reported by the registry). `verify-safety-report.mjs` then re-hashed every locked file against `target.json` and mechanically checked every finding's `file`, `line` and `snippet` against the tree's own text; it reported zero invalid findings across all six `findings/<department>.json` files (`--list-invalid` printed nothing for any of them).

Files actually read, aggregated across the six department reports: the entry point `server.js` end to end; every route module under `app/routes/` (`index.js`, `session.js`, `profile.js`, `contributions.js`, `benefits.js`, `allocations.js`, `research.js`, `memos.js`, `error.js`, `tutorial.js`); every DAO under `app/data/` (`user-dao.js`, `profile-dao.js`, `allocations-dao.js`, `benefits-dao.js`, `memos-dao.js`, `contributions-dao.js`, `research-dao.js`); the configuration tree `config/config.js` and `config/env/{all,development,production,test}.js`; the views that render request-derived data (`error-template.html`, `profile.html`, `memos.html`, `dashboard.html`, `benefits.html`, `login.html`, `allocations.html`, `research.html`); `package.json` and `package-lock.json`; `artifacts/db-reset.js` and `artifacts/cert/server.key`/`server.crt`; the deployment manifests `Dockerfile`, `docker-compose.yml`, `app.json`, `Procfile`, `.travis.yml`; `README.md`; `test/security/profile-test.js`; and the three `test/e2e/fixtures/users/*.json` fixtures. Each department's own `report/<department>.md` states its exact file list and method in full detail.

## Findings

### critical

- `data-001` — express-session secret is hard-coded in the committed configuration (`config/env/all.js:8`, CWE-798, confirmed) — anyone with the source can forge a session cookie for any user; read the secret from the environment with no fallback and rotate the committed value.
- `data-002` — The whole application, including login credentials and PII, is served over plain HTTP (`server.js:145`, CWE-319, confirmed) — any network-position attacker can read credentials, session cookies and SSN/bank data in transit and replay a captured cookie; terminate TLS and set the cookie `secure` flag.
- `dep-mongodb` — mongodb driver range resolves to a version pulling in a critical BSON deserialization advisory (`package.json:18`, CWE-1357, confirmed) — a server that deserializes attacker-influenced BSON can be crashed or have its deserialization logic abused with no authentication; move off the 2.x/3.1.x driver line to mongodb@7.6.0.
- `dep-swig` — swig template engine is pinned to its final, unmaintained release with an unfixed path-traversal advisory (`package.json:22`, CWE-1357, confirmed) — a template-path controlled by user input lets an attacker read arbitrary files the server can access; replace swig, since no upstream fix will ever ship.
- `dep-underscore` — underscore floating range resolves to a version with a critical arbitrary-code-execution advisory (`package.json:23`, CWE-1357, confirmed) — any reachable `_.template` call with attacker-influenced input executes arbitrary code; upgrade to underscore >=1.12.1.
- `dep-forever` — forever process supervisor resolves to its oldest possible version and pulls in a critical prototype-pollution chain (`package.json:15`, CWE-1357, confirmed) — prototype pollution in nconf/minimist beneath a production dependency can corrupt object behavior application-wide; upgrade to forever@4.0.3 or retire it.
- `injection-eval-pretax` — Server-side JavaScript injection via `eval()` on contribution percentage preTax (`app/routes/contributions.js:32`, CWE-95, confirmed) — any authenticated user can execute arbitrary JavaScript on the server; replace with `Number()`/`parseInt()` plus a NaN check.
- `injection-eval-aftertax` — Server-side JavaScript injection via `eval()` on contribution percentage afterTax (`app/routes/contributions.js:33`, CWE-95, confirmed) — same remote code execution as the preTax sink; replace `eval` with numeric parsing.
- `injection-eval-roth` — Server-side JavaScript injection via `eval()` on contribution percentage roth (`app/routes/contributions.js:34`, CWE-95, confirmed) — same remote code execution as the other two contribution-field sinks; replace `eval` with numeric parsing.
- `secrets-default-admin-password` — Default admin account is seeded with a hard-coded plaintext password that the login flow compares directly (`artifacts/db-reset.js:18`, CWE-798, confirmed) — anyone who reaches the login page of a deployment seeded from this repository can log in as the admin with `Admin_123`; never seed production with a fixed, publicly-known password.

### high

- `access-idor-allocations-userid` — Allocations page loads any employee's portfolio by trusting the userId URL parameter (`app/routes/allocations.js:16`, CWE-639, confirmed) — any authenticated employee can view any other employee's allocation by changing the URL's `:userId`; derive the id from `req.session`, not `req.params`.
- `access-benefits-post-missing-admin-check` — Any logged-in employee can change any other employee's benefit start date (`app/routes/index.js:56`, CWE-862, confirmed) — a low-privileged account can overwrite another employee's `benefitStartDate`; mount the already-written `isAdmin` middleware.
- `access-csrf-profile-post` — State-changing profile update has no CSRF protection (`app/routes/index.js:48`, CWE-352, confirmed) — a page a logged-in victim visits can silently rewrite their bank account/routing numbers, SSN or address; mount the already-imported `csurf()` middleware.
- `data-003` — User passwords are stored in the database in plaintext (`app/data/user-dao.js:25`, CWE-256, confirmed) — a DB compromise, backup leak or insider access recovers every real password; hash with bcrypt/argon2id and compare with the library's function, never `===`.
- `data-004` — SSN, date of birth and bank account/routing numbers are written to the database unencrypted (`app/data/profile-dao.js:61`, CWE-312, confirmed) — the same exposure paths yield every user's SSN, DOB and bank details; encrypt with AES-256-GCM and a key from a secrets manager before the `$set`.
- `dep-express` — express resolves to a version with its own XSS/open-redirect advisories and pulls in five more vulnerable packages (`package.json:13`, CWE-1357, confirmed) — an attacker can abuse `response.redirect()` or block the event loop via a vulnerable `path-to-regexp`/`qs`; upgrade express past 4.21.2.
- `dep-body-parser` — body-parser resolves to a version with two denial-of-service advisories (`package.json:9`, CWE-1357, confirmed) — a crafted URL-encoded or malformed-limit body can exhaust server resources pre-authentication; upgrade to >=1.20.6.
- `dep-marked` — marked is pinned to an exact version carrying six XSS/ReDoS advisories (`package.json:17`, CWE-1357, confirmed) — user-supplied Markdown reflected through marked can yield stored/reflected XSS or block the event loop; upgrade off the pre-1.0 marked line entirely.
- `dep-helmet` — helmet is six major versions behind current and pulls in two vulnerable transitive packages (`package.json:16`, CWE-1357, confirmed) — the module that emits this app's CSP header is itself flagged; upgrade to a current helmet major and rewrite the configuration for its v4+ API.
- `injection-nosql-where-allocations` — NoSQL injection via unsanitized threshold in MongoDB `$where` clause (`app/data/allocations-dao.js:78`, CWE-943, confirmed) — an attacker can run arbitrary JavaScript inside the MongoDB server via the `threshold` query parameter; never build `$where` from request input, use a plain query object.
- `injection-ssrf-research` — Server-side request forgery via unvalidated url/symbol query parameters (`app/routes/research.js:16`, CWE-918, confirmed) — an authenticated user can make the server fetch an internal or cloud-metadata address and read the response back; allowlist the destination host and block private/link-local ranges.
- `injection-redos-profile-bankrouting` — Regular-expression denial of service on bankRouting via catastrophic-backtracking pattern (`app/routes/profile.js:61`, CWE-1333, confirmed) — a crafted digit string pins the Node event loop and denies service to every user; remove the redundant outer quantifier and cap input length.
- `injection-xss-memos-marked` — Stored XSS: unescaped markdown-rendered memo output with autoescape disabled (`app/views/memos.html:31`, CWE-79, confirmed) — a planted memo executes script in every viewer's browser; re-enable swig autoescaping and sanitize marked's HTML output with an allowlist library.
- `platform-002-plaintext-http` — Server listens on plain HTTP only; HSTS and TLS are both disabled (`server.js:144`, CWE-319, confirmed) — session cookies, credentials and PII travel in cleartext; terminate TLS and enable HSTS once it is in place.
- `platform-003-session-cookie-flags` — Session cookie is issued with the default name, no httpOnly, no secure and no sameSite attribute (`server.js:78`, CWE-614, confirmed) — the cookie is capturable on the network and attached cross-site, widening CSRF exposure; set `secure`, `sameSite` and a non-default cookie name.
- `platform-004-csrf-disabled` — CSRF protection middleware is commented out even though views still submit a `_csrf` token field (`server.js:104`, CWE-352, confirmed) — every state-changing POST route accepts forged cross-site requests; mount `csurf()` and keep populating the token the templates already expect.
- `platform-005-session-fixation` — Login does not regenerate the session id, enabling session fixation (`app/routes/session.js:116`, CWE-384, confirmed) — an attacker who fixes a victim's pre-authentication session id inherits a fully authenticated session after the victim logs in; call `req.session.regenerate()` before setting `req.session.userId`, exactly as the signup handler in the same file already does.
- `platform-007-autoescape-disabled` — Template engine's autoescaping is globally disabled, and a profile field named "SafeString" is rendered unescaped (`server.js:135`, CWE-79, confirmed) — an attacker-supplied `firstName` is reflected unescaped into an attribute and an `href`, executing script in the viewer's session; re-enable `autoescape: true` and encode `firstName` for its specific output context.

### medium

- `access-benefits-get-missing-admin-check` — Benefits listing is reachable by any logged-in employee, not only admins (`app/routes/index.js:55`, CWE-862, confirmed) — any employee can enumerate every non-admin employee's id, name and benefit start date; mount the already-written `isAdmin` middleware.
- `access-password-compare-plaintext-noncontanttime` — Login compares passwords with plain `===` instead of a constant-time hash verify (`app/data/user-dao.js:61`, CWE-916, confirmed) — passwords are recoverable in the DB and login timing leaks a guess's correctness character by character; hash with bcrypt and compare with `bcrypt.compareSync`.
- `access-login-no-rate-limit` — Login endpoint has no attempt limit or lockout (`app/routes/index.js:34`, CWE-307, confirmed) — an attacker can submit unlimited password guesses per account; add a per-account counter with lockout or backoff.
- `access-session-no-expiration` — Session cookie has no maxAge, so sessions never expire server-side (`server.js:78`, CWE-613, confirmed) — a fixed, stolen or left-open session stays valid indefinitely; set an explicit `cookie.maxAge`.
- `data-005` — The merged application config, including the session-cookie secret, is printed to stdout on every start (`config/config.js:13`, CWE-532, confirmed) — anyone with log access reads the signing secret from the startup log; remove the log line or redact known-sensitive keys.
- `data-006` — Unhandled errors are rendered verbatim to the client (`app/routes/error.js:10`, CWE-209, confirmed) — a triggered backend error discloses internal error text to the requester; log server-side only and render a fixed generic message.
- `injection-open-redirect-learn` — Open redirect via unvalidated url query parameter (`app/routes/index.js:72`, CWE-601, confirmed) — a crafted `/learn?url=` link redirects a logged-in victim to an attacker-controlled site; validate the target against an allowlist or restrict it to relative paths.
- `injection-xss-profile-firstname-value` — Reflected XSS: unescaped firstName echoed into input value attribute (`app/views/profile.html:41`, CWE-79, confirmed) — a crafted `firstName` breaks out of the `value` attribute and executes script; enable autoescaping or attribute-encode the field.
- `injection-xss-profile-firstname-href` — Reflected XSS: unescaped firstName echoed into href attribute (`app/views/profile.html:78`, CWE-79, confirmed) — a `javascript:` URI in `firstName` executes on click; URL-encode the field for this specific context and reject non-http(s) schemes.
- `platform-001-missing-headers` — Helmet security-header middleware is entirely commented out; no CSP, frameguard, no-cache or X-Powered-By hardening is applied (`server.js:41`, CWE-693, confirmed) — every response is fingerprintable and unprotected against clickjacking, with no defense-in-depth against any XSS that lands; uncomment and mount helmet's header middleware.
- `platform-008-node-env-defaults-development` — Configuration defaults to the development environment whenever NODE_ENV is unset or empty (`config/config.js:5`, CWE-1188, confirmed) — a deployment that forgets to set NODE_ENV runs with a livereload script injected and secrets logged to stdout; fail closed instead of defaulting to development.
- `platform-009-open-redirect` — The /learn route redirects to a client-supplied URL with no validation (`app/routes/index.js:70`, CWE-601, confirmed) — the same open-redirect exposure as `injection-open-redirect-learn`, reported independently by the platform department at the route-declaration line; validate the redirect target against an allowlist.
- `platform-010-no-rate-limiting-login` — No rate limiting or account lockout on the login route (`app/routes/index.js:33`, CWE-307, confirmed) — an attacker can brute-force or enumerate usernames with no throttling; mount a rate limiter keyed by IP and by submitted username.
- `secrets-committed-rsa-private-key` — An RSA private key is committed to the repository as a TLS server key artifact (`artifacts/cert/server.key:1`, CWE-321, confirmed) — anyone with the repository holds the private key for any certificate ever paired with it; never commit key material, and rotate any certificate issued against this key.

### low

- `data-008` — A static encryption key for PII encryption is hard-coded in committed configuration (`config/env/all.js:9`, CWE-321, possible) — the disabled SSN/DOB encryption path would, if re-enabled as-is, encrypt every record with a key public in this repository; load the key from a secrets manager independent of that code path's status.
- `dep-bcrypt-nodejs` — Password-hashing dependency bcrypt-nodejs has had no release in over a decade and is registry-deprecated (`package.json:8`, CWE-1104, confirmed) — a future weakness in its salt/comparison logic will never be patched upstream; migrate to `bcrypt` or `bcryptjs`.
- `secrets-zap-api-key-default-config` — OWASP ZAP proxy API key is hard-coded and used as the default test/dev configuration (`config/env/development.js:6`, CWE-798, confirmed) — a committed credential reused as the tool's live default against a private test proxy; read it from an environment variable and rotate it.

### info

- `dep-node-esapi` — node-esapi, used for input encoding, was published once in 2014 and never updated since (`package.json:20`, CWE-1104, confirmed) — an unaudited, single-release encoding library carries unknown-but-nonzero risk with no advisory trail; replace it with an actively maintained, purpose-built encoding library.

## What was not covered

- **Git history.** Per the target being read-only from a single locked snapshot, no department ran `git log -p` or a history-wide secret scanner (gitleaks/trufflehog); a secret rotated in a prior commit would not be visible to this review.
- **Running the application.** No department started NodeGoat, ran the e2e/security test suites (`test/e2e/*`, `test/security/profile-test.js`) against a live instance, or performed dynamic testing, exploitation, or fuzzing; every finding is a static read of the source, not an observed behavior on the wire.
- **`node_modules` and the full transitive dependency tree.** `node_modules` is not part of the 111 locked files and was not reviewed; `package-lock.json` was used as npm audit's/npm view's input but not opened and read line by line for embedded registry URLs or credentials.
- **135 of the 145 npm-audit advisory entries** on transitive and dev-time (build/test tooling) packages were read only far enough to attribute them to the direct dependency that pulls them in; they were not each written up as their own finding, per the dependencies department's grouping choice.
- **Supply-chain provenance and typosquatting (CWE-829).** Direct dependency names were read for obvious lookalikes but no registry-history or maintainer-count check ran across the 1,479-package transitive graph.
- **Reachability of every dependency advisory into a live NodeGoat request path** (e.g. whether `_.template`, swig's file-loading, or marked's parser are actually reachable from an HTTP route with attacker data) was not traced by the dependencies department; that is source-level analysis, and the injection department traced only the sinks named in its own findings above.
- **Vendored front-end assets** (`app/assets/vendor/{bootstrap,jquery,morris,raphael,font-awesome,html5shiv}.*`) and `app/assets/js/*` were not read line by line for DOM-based sinks; they were only grepped for `postMessage`, `document.write`, `innerHTML` and `location.href`, and the application's own scripts (as opposed to vendored code) use none of them.
- **JWT.** NodeGoat uses `express-session` cookies exclusively; there is no JWT issuance or verification anywhere in the tree, so the JWT-specific checks in the access department's remit do not apply.
- **MFA.** The application has no multi-factor or device-remembering mechanism at all; this is a design gap noted here rather than filed as a line-level finding.
- **GitHub Actions mutable action-tag pinning.** `semgrep` flagged this in `.github/workflows/*.yml` in every department's scan run, and each department's report explicitly deferred it to another department (access → injection/secrets/platform; secrets → "left for other departments"), but no department actually filed it as a finding. It is disclosed here as a check that ran and produced a real lead that fell through the cracks between departments rather than being evaluated and either filed or explicitly ruled out.
- **`.github/workflows/*.yml` secrets usage** beyond the mutable-tag pattern (e.g. a secret printed to a workflow log) was read once by the secrets department but not exhaustively traced line by line.
- **Runtime/deployment configuration.** The actual environment variables, reverse-proxy or WAF configuration, and whether `NODE_ENV=production` is set in any real deployment are outside what a static read can confirm; every header, cookie, and TLS finding above describes what the code would do, not what a specific live deployment does.
- **Memory-safety and timing-side-channel bug classes** were not checked; this is a pure Node/JS application reviewed for logic-level issues only.
- **`access-session-fixation-login`** (dropped from `findings.json`) — duplicates `platform-005-session-fixation` at the same `app/routes/session.js:116`, CWE-384. Both departments independently traced the same missing `req.session.regenerate()` call with `confirmed` confidence; since confidence was tied, the higher-severity assessment (platform's `high`) was kept over the lower one (access's `medium`).
- **`data-007`** (dropped from `findings.json`) — duplicates `secrets-default-admin-password` at the same `artifacts/db-reset.js:18`, CWE-798. Both were `confirmed`; since confidence was tied, the higher-severity assessment (secrets' `critical`) was kept over the lower one (data's `medium`).
- **`secrets-cookie-secret`** (dropped from `findings.json`) — duplicates `data-001` at the same `config/env/all.js:8`, CWE-798. Both were `confirmed`; since confidence was tied, the higher-severity assessment (data's `critical`) was kept over the lower one (secrets' `high`).
- **`platform-006-error-handler-exposes-error`** (dropped from `findings.json`) — duplicates `data-006` at the same `app/routes/error.js:10`, CWE-209. Both were `confirmed` at the same `medium` severity; with confidence and severity fully tied, `data-006` was kept as the deterministic (alphabetically first) tie-break.
- **`secrets-crypto-key-dead-code`** (dropped from `findings.json`) — duplicates `data-008` at the same `config/env/all.js:9`, CWE-321. Both were `possible` at the same `low` severity; with confidence and severity fully tied, `data-008` was kept as the deterministic (alphabetically first) tie-break.

`verify-safety-report.mjs` mechanically confirmed, against the locked tree above: every one of the 46 findings in `findings.json` resolves to an existing file and line, with its `snippet` matching the tree's own text at that line; every finding id is unique; no two findings share `file`, `line` and `cwe`; and every `file:line` citation written anywhere in this document resolves in the same locked tree.
