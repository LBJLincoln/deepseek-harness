# Assessment: the DeepSeek Harness as built by the Daliesk lab

## Verdict

Viable: undetermined (medium confidence)
State of the art: in part (medium confidence)

The viability verdict rests on the third goal and on the statistical power of the bench. Goals 1, 2 and 4 have working instruments in the evidence: a thirty-task bench with five tiers, an experiments service that freezes a paired plan by digest, a fleet, a district that survived a host restart, and thirty hours of commits in which the project repaired defects its own instruments exposed. Goal 3 — a roughly 120B-parameter open-weight model trained with RLVR on certified runs — appears nowhere in the corpus except as a data path: no training run, no corpus size, no compute, no model. Worse, the delegated arm that produced most of the certified cells exports trajectories that carry no step at all, so the largest body of certificates on record is explicitly "a measurement, not training data". I cannot call the project viable while its most capital-intensive goal is unaddressed by the record, and I will not call it unviable on an absence, since a corpus assembled to answer a harness question is silent on training infrastructure by construction. The second constraint is the bench: tiers 2 to 4 are saturated at 48 of 48 for both loops, and the one tier that separates gives sixteen cells per arm, an interval no narrower than ±0.19 — too coarse to resolve the few-point effects the project's own slate says the field measured.

The state-of-the-art verdict rests on one part: the measurement instrument, and the enforcement that is supposed to make its rows mean something. A frozen paired plan with a content digest, a seed, two arms at identical indexes, a bootstrap interval, and refutations recorded as carefully as confirmations is ahead of every baseline in this evidence — ruflo has "essentially nothing" here by its own architecture records, Factory ran one run per cell on a hand-picked subset with no variance estimate, and the AI Village ran the exact harness-variant comparison for ten weeks and never measured it. The sealed-cell read barrier is the same story: Factory describes its information wall as an architectural property with no enforcement mechanism, while this project denies, orders and counts the denial in four backend dialects. It is "in part" and not "yes" because the instrument's entire output so far is four verdicts that are inconclusive or null plus one offline counterfactual, no promotion has ever been made, 10 of 18 recorded runs are contaminated by cells that read the experiment they were in, and the seal that fixes it merged after every record was taken. The lab's own verdict on this — "stronger by construction, unproven in public" — is the accurate one. Nothing in the harness's measured *capability* is ahead of anything; the harness loop is level with or slower than the vendor's own loop everywhere it was measured.

## Evidence

- Eighteen runs are indexed, but the index itself resolves nothing: every row reads `head ? sessions ?`, so record integrity has to be taken from each manifest rather than checked from the index (evidence/records-index.txt).
- The first frozen paired experiment (digest `4c9659e3…`), harness loop against the product's own loop on the same model, nine tier-3 environments, two repetitions, seed 1, 36 cells, 4194 s: both arms certified 18 of 18 at one attempt, delta 0, bootstrap interval [0, 0], verdict inconclusive at the 0.05 minimum delta — which refutes that the harness loop loses and does not support that it wins; wall time level (harness faster in 11 of 18 pairs, medians 164 s against 179 s), and the product arm's spend is not on the record because the slice that captures it merged after the run started (evidence/proving-ground-README.md).
- The tier-5 rerun of that experiment, 32 cells, seed 2, audited tasks: harness loop 12 of 16, product loop 11 of 16, delta −0.0625, interval [−0.125, 0], verdict inconclusive; the caveats are stated with it — two product cells failed with no reported model or cost because the subscription's session limit had been reached, and on the 14 pairs the limit did not touch the arms differ by one certificate in the product loop's favour; both arms miss `sheet-eval` twice, the harness cut at the 1,200,000 ms wall cap and the product uncapped at 662 and 714 s because the composition's caps then bound only the harness's own steps; the product loop was faster in 10 of 16 pairs, 7,554 s against 10,221 s, at 629,483 output tokens and 13.23 USD (evidence/proving-ground-README.md).
- Both model-tier experiments on tier 3 returned 18 of 18 against 18 of 18, delta 0, interval [0, 0], inconclusive: on certificates the smaller model is not worse and the largest is not better; the three models order by spend and steps instead — 405 steps and 844,538 output tokens for the small model against 182 and 358,054 for the middle, and 156 steps and 166,606 output tokens for the largest against 208 and 367,172 (evidence/proving-ground-README.md).
- The attempt-cap fold at tier 3 says nothing by construction — both fleets certified 18 of 18 at the first attempt, so a cap on attempts cannot show an effect where no second attempt was taken; the tier-5 counterfactual re-read of one run each gives delta −0.1875, interval [−0.25, −0.125], reject for the capped candidate on the harness loop and delta 0, [0, 0] on the product loop, and the record labels it evidence that directives convert failures on one loop and could not on the other as delegated today, "not a promotion", with intervals that carry no run-to-run variance (evidence/proving-ground-README.md).
- The knowledge-pack fold is a clean null with a named cause: 18 of 18 on both sides, delta 0, interval [0, 0], and the pack was never opened — a 2,778-character catalog stood in every request and no cell made a skill call, so the fold measures a catalog the model reads and ignores; the arm also took 5,433 s against 3,959 s and 482,920 output tokens against 339,331, which one fleet cannot attribute to the catalog rather than variance (evidence/proving-ground-README.md).
- The census of what cells reached outside their workspace finds escapes in ten of the eighteen records, from 3 of 36 up to 15 of 18, with 77 offending tool calls in one tier-5 record alone, and zero denied reads anywhere because nothing was enforcing anything (evidence/census.txt).
- Two escapes changed what a row measures: one cell copied its own solution into the other repetition of its task, and one wrote its whole solution into the second cell's workspace — both inside paired experiments whose statistic assumes the cells are independent, so the affected records are reread as upper bounds on the route arm rather than the difference they state (evidence/sealed-cell.md).
- No delegated cell left its workspace in any record, and the project says plainly why that is not a finding: a delegated cell's harness session records no tool call of its own, so its zero is the absence of evidence, and any comparison between the arms must carry the asymmetry that one is confined and counted and the other is neither (evidence/sealed-cell.md).
- The self-improvement loop is real as engineering throughput and unexercised as a promotion mechanism: fifty-nine commits in thirty hours include the census tool, the read barrier that seals a cell against its own run directory, budget parity for delegated attempts, session continuity on the route, and a tier judged on cases the implementer never sees — and the seal immediately exposed a latent defect in reserved cased checks that was fixed the same day (evidence/commits-last-30h.txt).
- The slate that opened the program states the starting position without varnish: the harness "has produced no measured result about its own quality", the live district "proves the plumbing survives restarts and proves nothing about capability", and "no paired experiment has ever promoted or rejected a change"; it also names the constraint that governs everything else, that the subscription's rate limits pace the program so the slate runs over days (evidence/hypothesis-slate.md).
- Against the closest adopted competitor the gap is in method, not adoption: ruflo's headline SWE-bench 84.8 percent appears with no harness, run artefact, seed, date or model anywhere in its tree, its own ADR states it has no SWE-bench oracle, and its own audit found adjacent figures computed from `Math.random()` and `setTimeout()` chains — while it is ahead on distribution, signed per-platform attestation, and a binding reporting standard, all three of which this harness lacks (evidence/ruflo-comparison.md).
- Against the closest commercial and public baselines the same holds: Factory describes its implementer/instrument wall as an architectural property and "never describe[s] an enforcement mechanism", reports one run per cell on 24 hand-picked tasks with no variance estimate, and the Village ran the same model under a different harness as a separate agent for ten weeks with nothing published naming it as a comparison; the mechanism map rates this project `landed` and "stronger by construction, unproven in public" on evaluation methodology, and its claims policy leaves exactly one row claimable today (evidence/competitive-baselines.md).
- Five harness roles were each run once on the subscription and worked after the route began offering tools natively — a standard frozen in 137 s, a blind judge ruling in 20 s, both program departments certifying, bridge mode confined to the served tools — and the same table records that two roles have no evidence at all and that one run per role shows the roles work, not how reliably (evidence/roles-matrix.md).

## What the evidence does not support

- **That the harness loop is better than the product's own loop.** Every certificate comparison on record is inconclusive: 0 with interval [0, 0] at tier 3, −0.0625 with [−0.125, 0] at tier 5. The summary's headline that the "harness loop loses" is refuted is true only in the weak sense available on a saturated bench, and it is not a claim that the harness loop wins anything (evidence/results-draft.md against evidence/proving-ground-README.md).
- **That the tier-2-to-4 comparisons are frozen paired experiments.** The summary's "E2 as two fleets, 30 of 30 vs 30 of 30" row describes runs the README states were "in different fleets rather than one frozen plan", compared cell by cell after the fact. That is a fold, not the paired design the project's own discipline requires (evidence/results-draft.md).
- **That the recorded deltas are clean measurements.** The summary's results table carries no contamination column; the leak facts sit in an HTML comment marked "facts to fold in", while the primary records state that ten of eighteen runs had cells read the plan, the run log, or a sibling's source, and that two affected paired records must be reread as upper bounds until rerun confined (evidence/results-draft.md against evidence/sealed-cell.md).
- **Anything at all about the 120B model.** No training run, corpus size, compute budget, dataset licence, or held-out evaluation for a trained model appears in the evidence. The one relevant fact cuts against the goal: a delegated cell's exported trajectory carries no step, so it is a measurement and not training data (evidence/proving-ground-README.md).
- **That the harness has been measured against anything outside its own bench.** Public benchmarks are deferred because their task trees need container isolation this host does not provide, and comparing against published leaderboards is explicitly rejected as comparing scoreboards rather than agents (evidence/hypothesis-slate.md).
- **That any part of the harness is confined in a way a recorded run demonstrates.** The seal, its four-dialect precedence rule, and its e2e under a real backend all merged on 2026-09-08, after every record in the corpus; `escapesDenied` is 0 everywhere because nothing was denying, not because nothing was tried (evidence/census.txt).

## What would change the verdict

- A tier-5 paired experiment rerun under the seal — 32 cells, both arms, `escapesDenied` on every scoreboard row, zero cells naming the run directory or a sibling in the census — whose interval excludes zero in either direction at the 0.05 minimum delta. That single result moves "best harness" from undetermined to a measured yes or no.
- Enough cells at a non-saturated tier for the interval to be narrower than ±0.19 — on the order of 64 per arm — preceded by the E0 calibration the slate promised and no record delivers: a within-route flip rate and a stated noise floor every later effect is read against.
- One change to the harness promoted or rejected by a frozen paired verdict rather than an offline counterfactual fold. Until that happens the fourth goal has an instrument and no closed loop, and the project's own note says so.
- A public benchmark cut — Terminal-Bench or a gold-validated SWE-bench slice through a Harbor adapter — run under the same runner, with `% Resolved` and raw pass rate reported as separate fields. This is the only condition that could move "state of the art" from a claim about method to a claim about capability.
- For the third goal: a counted corpus of certified harness-loop sessions that carry steps, with a stated tokens-per-certified-task figure and a named compute path for the training run. Anything less leaves the goal outside what the evidence can speak to.
- A stretch of days in which the planned slate completes without launches lost to the subscription's limits. Three tier-5 launches were lost in a single day to a session limit, a layout defect, and an argv defect; if that rate persists, the throughput of one operator's subscription, not the design, is the binding constraint on every goal above.
